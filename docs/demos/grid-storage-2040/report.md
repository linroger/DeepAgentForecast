LFP still dominates the four-hour segment, but its territory in the stack above 8 hours is gradually eroded—partitioned among pumped hydro, compressed air, flow batteries, thermal storage, iron-air, and liquid air.

**The Starting Contrast: Delivery Has Exceeded Expectations, but the Market Has Still Not Priced by Duration**

In the official statistics of China's National Energy Administration (NEA), of the 136 GW / 351 GWh cumulative "new-type storage" capacity at the end of 2025, projects above four hours account for only 27.6%, the average duration is 2.58 hours, and lithium-ion accounts for 96.1% of the power share [S68]. In other words: installed capacity has already exceeded the original baseline, but the structure has not been rearranged by duration, and the price signal still favors four-hour lithium.

The two reference frames point in the same direction for cumulative volume—but the former is expected deployment under the baseline scenario, the latter is a normative net-zero requirement, so the two figures should not be treated as equivalent.

**The First Gate to Pricing by Duration: Capacity Markets at FERC, ERCOT, and PJM**

The central mechanism of Scenario I is that several mutually independent capacity markets almost simultaneously incorporate "duration" as a separate compensated dimension.

The Real-Time Co-optimization Plus Batteries (RTC+B) revision (effective December 2025) sets a new clearing benchmark and may push a significant portion of ERCOT's pure-battery storage projects away from pure market exposure and toward capacity-backed, tolling-style procurement. [S239]

This policy combination transforms China's role from a backstop market that absorbs unlimited LFP into a laboratory for "paying by duration and dispatching by duration"—the policy foundation for Scenario I on the China side.

**The Second Gate to Pricing by Duration: DOE Liftoff and the European/Gulf Contract Chain**

This is the regime's structural backbone. It is Chinese-financed, Chinese-integrated, and Chinese-warranted — though increasingly built outside China's borders.

### Three transmission rails: NDRC enabling, OBBBA exclusion, provincial compression

The first rail is NDRC's enabling posture toward Chinese cell and integration champions. NDRC and NEA together set a ≥180 GW new-type storage target by 2027 with RMB 250 billion of direct investment under Document 1360 (the New-Type Storage Scaling Action Plan), reframing storage as a dispatchable system resource that must be "observable, measurable, controllable," and explicitly pushing compressed-air, flow batteries, and sodium-ion toward commercialization [S28]. NDRC's enabling clauses for CATL and BYD translate into capital allocation, permit acceleration, and explicit demonstration-cohort underwriting. CATL's 40 GWh Fujian sodium-ion line — the single largest disclosed sodium-ion expansion at US$735 million — is an example of how NDRC's commercialization mandate pairs with anchor tenant capacity. [S40] On the storage side, the same action plan assigns compressed-air, flow, and sodium-ion to commercialization tracks while keeping thermal, hydrogen, gravity, and solid-state at demonstration scale — a sequencing decision that concentrates Chinese export capacity on lithium-ion-family products and reserves non-lithium routes for domestic demonstration.

The second rail is OBBBA's FEOC exclusion. FEOC rules effectively force US project sponsors and LFP cell makers to decertify Chinese-controlled suppliers across anode, cathode, and pack stages — operationally difficult given Chinese dominance of upstream LFP supply nodes. The OBBBA Treasury Notice and its implementing storage ITC provisions are characterized by California Energy Storage Alliance summaries as "legally unsettled," which under SCN-C stalls capacity construct adoption in the West. This bifurcation — US market structurally expensive and politically walled off from Chinese content; ex-US markets relatively open — is the precise condition that allows Chinese integrators to retain ex-China share even as their US-market share collapses. The same constraint does not bind Saudi Arabia, the GCC, India, Australia (for utility-scale), Latin America, or Africa, where Chinese banks and EPC contractors still compete on integrated price.

The third rail is the State Grid / Southern Grid lead-time compression. In this regime, China's 2027 NEA installed-base print versus the 180 GW target is the cleanest single signal for whether SCN-C is on track, with any miss sharply de-rating the trajectory. [S34]

## Scenario III — Lithium Dominance, Extended (16%): LFP Plus Sodium-Ion, No Real LDES Liftoff

### Why LFP Plus Sodium-Ion Holds the Floor

The first leg of the argument rests on the scale economics LFP has already accumulated. The dispute between these two figures, one global, one US-specific, one LCOE, one LCOS, is itself diagnostic: LFP's marginal cost curve has fallen so steeply that the binding constraint has shifted from cell price to interconnection, duration accreditation, contracted revenue, and conversion of announced projects into operating assets.

On top of that floor, sodium-ion adds an incremental extension rather than a structural alternative. CATL disclosed a 40 GWh sodium-ion expansion in Fujian, and HiNa has described sodium-ion as "entering the commercial era". [S40] Vendors report nameplate capacity, production plans, and framework agreements; skeptical grid operators require commissioned projects, accepted cells, independent performance data, and bankable warranties.

### Why Non-Lithium LDES Stays Below the Threshold

The second leg of the argument concerns the cost trajectory of competing long-duration routes. These are not aspirational numbers; they are the LDES Council's own published benchmarks for the technologies with the most advanced commercial reference projects.

The DOE Liftoff pathway for LDES articulates these targets but describes them as a pathway, not as achieved performance [S88]. In a world where LFP costs keep declining and the four-hour revenue stack is still lucrative in markets that compensate duration weakly, the marginal investment dollar continues to flow toward proven LFP at four hours rather than toward flow, CAES, thermal, iron-air, or liquid-air at eight-to-twelve hours.

Form Energy's iron-air chemistry, widely considered the leading multi-day LDES candidate, illustrates the binding constraint: despite high-profile demonstration projects and significant DOE funding, Form's commissioned fleet remains in the tens of MWh, and no iron-air project has yet crossed the 100 MWh operational threshold with bankable warranty data. [S98] Hydrostor's compressed-air projects in Canada and Australia and Highview Power's liquid-air projects in the UK and the US have advanced from demonstration toward commercial pilots, but cumulative commissioned capacity across these routes remains well below 1 GWh per technology per year. Vanadium-redox flow batteries from Sumitomo, Invinity, and Chinese vendors have shipped tens of MWh of grid-connected capacity in 2024–2025, but the largest single commissioned vanadium-flow project remains under 100 MWh, and total cumulative installed flow-battery GWh globally has not reached the threshold required for cost-curve commerciality. [S9]

A useful counterfactual: pumped hydro, the only non-lithium technology with a multi-decade commercial track record, continues to expand in China through State Grid and Southern Grid procurement cycles, with Hubei and Hebei PHES projects among the notable developments. PHES is included in the non-lithium share by most definitional conventions. But PHES's commercial scaling is driven by Chinese state planning, not by merchant cost competition; it follows a different deployment logic from flow, CAES, and iron-air, and its share of incremental global additions is structurally limited by siting, civil works lead times of seven to ten years, and the same interconnection and grid-connection constraints that bind battery storage.

### Capacity-Market Design as the Brake on Duration

The third leg of the argument is the capacity-market and accreditation architecture in the United States and in merchant markets more broadly, which continues to compensate megawatts rather than megawatt-hours. Yet the auction's accreditation methodology continues to recognize four-hour battery stacks at near-nameplate capacity credit, while eight- and ten-hour stacks receive diminishing marginal recognition beyond the four-hour cliff. Until PJM, ISO-NE, and ERCOT independently compensate duration, the merchant incentive to build eight- or twelve-hour assets remains weak relative to the four-hour LFP default.

ERCOT is the cleanest stress test. Post-RTC+B settlement, battery revenue has been pressured by ancillary-service saturation and merchant cannibalization as more identical four-hour assets chase the same energy and ancillary stack. In that world, the market signal that would tell a developer to build an eight-hour iron-air or a twelve-hour flow stack simply does not arrive.

The combined effect of FEOC constraints, duration-blind accreditation, and merchant revenue compression is to make the four-hour LFP stack the unique global risk-adjusted optimum for the entire decade.

International parallels reinforce rather than offset this pattern. Italy's MACSE capacity mechanism, often cited as a counter-example because it explicitly procures long-duration storage, has struggled to clear at technology-neutral price levels that would justify commercial non-lithium financing; The European Commission's storage policy emphasizes deployment and supply-chain diversification without yet mandating duration-accredited capacity procurement at scale [S92].

### Why This Scenario Is Still Only 16%

Second, regulatory and procurement experimentation is accelerating in directions that favor duration even where it is not yet decisive. CPUC's clean and affordable electricity procurement decision explicitly addresses resource-adequacy policy in ways that reward duration and availability rather than nameplate megawatts [S81]. The NDRC's 2025 guidance (Document 1360) reframes storage as a dispatchable system resource subject to telemetry, control, and utilization requirements, moving China away from build mandates toward dispatchable and market-compensated operation [S28]. The NDRC/NEA New-Type Storage Scaling Action Plan, with its 180 GW 2027 target and RMB 250 billion direct investment envelope, explicitly lists compressed-air, flow, and sodium-ion among the technology commercialization priorities [S34]. China's NEA reported 136 GW and 351 GWh of operational new-type storage at end-2025, with lithium-ion at 96.1% share and average duration of 2.58 hours, but also noted that the four-hour-plus share had reached 27.6% [S68]. 4. Reaching this threshold means the IRR tipping point for EU-domestic LFP capacity buildout has been crossed; the credibility of the "China-Centric Vertical" scenario in the EU market declines, and "LDES Diversification" gains additional support inside the EU.

The existence of the three structural constraints—safety compliance, recycling obligations, and supply chain concentration—means that the 2026–2040 storage industry is not a race to determine "which chemistry wins," but a race to determine which combination of "supply chain + market rules + safety/compliance cost structure" can first move multi-duration storage into project finance. ## Market Design as the Pivot: Capacity Accreditation, Interconnection, Compensation

**Market Design as the Pivot: Capacity Accreditation, Interconnection, and Compensation**

Three decades of merchant LFP in front-of-meter applications have made one lesson unambiguous: a megawatt-hour that cannot clear interconnection, cannot qualify for capacity accreditation, or cannot earn a duration-weighted payment will not be financed, regardless of how cheap the cells become.

### The interconnection gate: FERC Order 2023, cluster reform, and the OBBBA overlay

The United States is the cleanest laboratory for the queue problem because the data is granular and the reforms are dated. FERC Order 2023 replaced the first-come, first-served serial study process with cluster studies, milestone-based withdrawal penalties, up-front deposits, and maturity scoring — a structural redesign intended to clear the speculative volume that had grown to dominate the queue [S84]. The Order 2222 distributed-energy-resource aggregation architecture, by which front-of-meter and behind-the-meter assets may clear capacity, energy, and ancillary-service markets under a single participation model, sits beside Order 2023 as a related reform [S26]; its attribution to a single FERC docket number should be regarded as indicative rather than verified pending primary-source confirmation. The order pair is the principal US mechanism for translating equipment-cost declines into commissioned capacity.

The interaction with the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) makes the queue reform more binding, not less.

The China stack is moving in the opposite direction on interconnection but the same direction on discipline. The NDRC and NEA September 2025 New-Type Storage Scaling Action Plan locks a ≥180 GW new-type storage target by 2027 with RMB 250 billion of direct investment, reframes storage as a dispatchable system resource, and explicitly pushes compressed-air, flow, and sodium-ion toward commercialization while keeping solid-state, gravity, thermal, and hydrogen at demonstration scale [S34]. State Grid and Southern Grid, as the dominant connection authorities, retain discretion over telemetry acceptance, dispatch commands, and equivalent utilization measurement — the Chinese parallel to a cluster-study gate. China's reform is therefore not "more interconnection" but "interconnection conditional on observable, measurable, controllable operation," which functions as an internal version of the same FERC Order 2023 discipline. [S84]

The European analogue is the network code and cap-floor regime rather than a cluster study. Whether that trajectory clears the bankability gate is therefore not a function of cell supply, which is now mostly Chinese, but of whether EU member-state capacity mechanisms and Italian MACSE produce regulated cashflows of the kind that can absorb the regulatory perimeter cost.

### Compensation as the duration switch: PJM, ERCOT, ISO-NE, and the cannibalization problem

The narrowness of that cleared volume is itself diagnostic: most of the 134,311 MW procured cleared into capacity resources other than four-hour batteries, which means the auction's headline clearing price set the price but did not clear very many batteries at it. [S47]

The ERCOT experience is the more informative case because it is already a saturated four-hour market.

The deeper lesson of the ERCOT collapse is that arbitrage and ancillary credits alone no longer underwrite US four-hour LFP IRRs once congestion revenue is normalized, and the duration lever — not the energy arbitrage lever — is what has to be repriced if eight- to twelve-hour assets are to be financed. CAISO's RA counting rules differentiate 8-hour resources and accelerate iron-flow battery deployment relative to 4-hour-only assets.

Until those two reforms are synchronized — until a project can clear interconnection quickly and earn a duration-weighted capacity payment — the LFP four-hour plus merchant cannibalization tug will dominate US deployment. Without those payment rails, the cost targets are aspirational, not commercial.

### The regulated cashflow stack: NDRC Document 1360, Shandong, MACSE, and UK cap-and-floor

The NDRC/NEA October 2025 reform established a national capacity payment for new-type storage beginning with a pilot in Shandong, reframing storage as a dispatchable system resource with telemetry and control mandates that make non-compliant LFP assets removable from market registration [S28].

Italy's MACSE auction is the cleanest European expression of the same logic. UK NESO's cap-and-floor mechanism is the parallel for ≥8-hour projects, offering regulated upside and downside around a regulated floor that supports project finance for capital-heavy LDES technologies. the evidence base identifies the joint MACSE plus UK cap-and-floor stack as a credible mechanism that, if implemented at scale, would trigger the LDES Diversified base case for non-lithium long-duration storage — a strikingly direct statement about the leverage of market design over equipment cost.

The pattern that emerges across the regulated markets is consistent: duration, not energy, is the priced attribute, and the price takes the form of a multi-year capacity payment rather than an arbitrage capture. In the merchant markets — ERCOT, CAISO day-ahead, Australia NEM — duration remains an unpriced attribute, which is why merchant four-hour LFP saturates while merchant eight-hour assets cannot clear debt service.

The reverse view deserves explicit weight. It is possible that the LFP plus merchant cannibalization tug is not a transitional problem but a steady-state outcome, and that the regulated cashflow stack in Italy, the UK, China, and Saudi Arabia remains too small relative to global deployment to re-price duration at scale.

## Risk Landscape and Decision Implications for Investors, Policymakers, and Operators

### Upside, Downside, and Basis Risks Across the 2026–2040 Path

Each decision vector examined below threads back to one specific question: in a world where BloombergNEF's NEO 2026 frames cumulative station-scale storage climbing toward multi-hundred-gigawatt and multi-terawatt milestones across the 2026–2035 horizon, where Chinese integrators dominate the global BESS market with the majority of the top players China-based, and where the IEA Net Zero Emissions pathway sets an approximate 1,500 GW grid-scale storage requirement by 2030, what should investors, policymakers, and operators actually do with the next fourteen years of decisions? [S11] The honest answer is that deployment economics, technology readiness, and policy enforcement are all binding at different points — and the error modes are different for each role.

### What Investors Actually Have to Decide

But that cost collapse also means incremental capacity additions are now revenue-cannibalized rather than margin-accretive.

The investment question therefore splits three ways. **Manufacturing capacity additions in Chinese LFP are now a volume business with thin margins and rising trade exposure**, where OBBBA's Foreign Entity of Concern restrictions and the EU Battery Regulation's carbon-footprint, performance, and due-diligence disclosure obligations create at least three regional price regimes rather than one global cost curve. Energy Dome's CO2-cycle LAES competes at the 4–8 hour window using standard pressure vessels rather than cryogenic tanks, with TRL 7–8 and best-fit duration matching its Carrington UK demonstration;

**Project finance structures with versus without long-tenor contracts.** The financing arithmetic is now bifurcated. Without that contract, the same projects sink.

### What Policymakers Have to Decide

If ELCC accreditation caps at ≤6h, then 8–12h storage fails to monetize duration and remains a niche technology even after capital costs fall.

**Interconnection queue reform is the second lever.** FERC's Order 2023 imposed cluster-study reform with stricter milestone-based withdrawal penalties, up-front deposits, and maturity scoring to address the US interconnection-queue backlog. [S84] The European Commission's storage policy posture and Italian MACSE's technology-neutral capacity auction complement UK cap-and-floor by converting multi-hour storage from merchant exposure to regulated long-dated cashflows.

NMC's residual-material economics (cobalt and nickel value) make LFP recycling a compliance cost rather than a profit center under that regime. The policy trade-off is therefore between FDRE-style requirements that reward duration and accelerate non-lithium, and 4-hour MW bias that preserves LFP economics — and the choice is largely made by capacity-market design rather than by storage-specific legislation.

**The dispatchable-resource reframe.** China's NDRC/NEA Document 1360 of November 2025 explicitly reframes storage as a dispatchable system resource, mandates that stations be "observable, measurable, controllable" and follow dispatch commands, and provides that non-compliant LFP assets will be removed from market registration. [S28] The September 2025 New-Type Storage Scaling Action Plan locks the ≥180 GW target by 2027 with RMB 250 billion of direct investment, and the same document pushes compressed-air, flow batteries, and sodium-ion toward commercialization in 2025–2027 while keeping solid-state, gravity, thermal, and hydrogen at demonstration scale. [S34] For policymakers in Washington, Brussels, New Delhi, and Riyadh, the Chinese experiment is essentially: build mandates do not buy operating capacity; dispatchable reframe plus capacity compensation does.

### What Operators Have to Decide

**Dispatchable compliance is no longer optional.** China's 2025–2027 Action Plan and the November 2025 Document 1360 require connected storage to meet telemetry, dispatch, safety, and market-registration standards enforced through grid operators. [S28] non-compliant LFP assets are removed from market registration rather than given a soft warning. The implication for operators is that the cost of sitting idle during a dispatch signal is now loss of market access, not just foregone revenue.

**Safety compliance is binding for siting.** The Moss Landing fire of January 2025 and the Gateway Energy Camino fire continue to shape the 2026 edition of NFPA 855, raising compliance costs for stationary storage and constraining LFP deployment under SCN-C conditions. California's county-level setback standards and the California Energy Storage Alliance's joint BESS safety recommendations on spacing, deflagration venting, and water supply have effectively become a global compliance floor together with UL 9540A and IFC Section 1207. [S58] India's Central Electricity Authority published Draft Safety and Electric Supply Amendment Regulations in August 2025 that extend large-format BESS obligations to electrical safety, telemetry, and emergency-response training, raising development costs but potentially clarifying insurance and performance regimes. [S281] Operators investing in 2026–2030 must underwrite these costs as system costs, not as discretionary overhead — the same projects that cannot get insurance without NFPA 855 compliance cannot be financed at investment-grade spreads. [S66]

For grid-scale battery operators, the equivalent risk is interconnection-queue attrition under FERC Order 2023: even at BNEF's roughly US$266/kWh US four-hour turnkey cost under a 54% Section 301 tariff scenario, project IRR depends on whether queued positions survive cluster studies and milestone-based withdrawal penalties. [S84] The operator decision under SCN-D Stagnation risk is whether to commit to merchant or contracted revenue at the same time as committing to specialized siting — both decisions lock for the asset life.

the 2025–2027 Action Plan reframes capacity compensation around dispatch-following rather than installed capacity. [S9] Operators must therefore plan for a regime in which capacity payment eligibility depends on demonstrated availability through telemetry, dispatch compliance audits, and utilization above a regulator-set threshold. Saudi Arabia's 15-year Storage Services Agreements convert merchant risk into contracted revenue in the same way India's 15-year contracts and Shandong's provincial pilot do, separating revenue from energy arbitrage and lowering effective cost of capital. Operators who treat contracted-cashflow capacity constructs as the default — rather than as a hedge — will outperform those who anchor on merchant arbitrage and price cannibalization.

### The Three Signals That Will De-Risk or Destabilize the Base Case

Three near-term signals carry disproportionate weight because each of them independently converts Scenario B's base case into either Scenario A (Lithium Dominance, Extended) or Scenario C (China-Centric Vertical), and any of them arriving materially changes the deployment band against which investor, policymaker, and operator decisions are calibrated.

The Treasury's implementing notice is the legally binding document; A Treasury Notice that softens FEOC enforcement accelerates the China-Centric Vertical path; a strict Notice that retroactively withdraws FEOC waivers freezes ex-China deployments but locks CATL and BYD into Saudi, GCC, Belt-and-Road, and Latin American projects.

The signal is not the precise installed-base number but whether the equivalent utilization moves above the 1,195-hour 2025 baseline as the Shandong capacity-compensation pilot scales and as the dispatchable-compliance regime kicks non-compliant assets out of market registration. [S68] If attainment comes with rising utilization and duration creep toward four hours, the SCN-C China-Centric Vertical path validates; if attainment comes with deteriorating utilization and stranded assets, the SCN-D Stagnation path for China begins to materialize, and the global deployment band compresses materially.

### Basis Risks That Bind All Three Decision Vectors

Three second-order risks cut across roles. Any investment or policy thesis that treats equipment cost as the independent variable will misread the deployment band.

Those who reconcile differently will see the same world describe three different scenario mixes.
