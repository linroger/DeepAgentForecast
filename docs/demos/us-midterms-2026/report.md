## Part 2 — Framework & Synthesis

### Framework Overview

The 2026 midterm forecast is built on a four-scenario spine (46/24/18/11) that reflects a single organizing principle: a structurally unfavorable environment for the incumbent party is the base condition, but the cycle's resolution is dominated by a small number of discrete shocks between August and November. The framework treats the structural environment as the prior and the trigger sequence as the posterior update.

Four structural inputs define the prior. (i) Presidential approval: Trump at 35–37% across AP-NORC, Reuters/Ipsos, Quinnipiac, and Marist, with sub-40% on inflation and the economy. (ii) Generic ballot: D+5 to D+7, an unusually wide margin for a midterm-cycle environment. (iii) Special-election overperformance: DCCC-district Democrats overperformed 2024 baselines by roughly D+17.1 in 2025–26 specials, the strongest aggregate special-election signal for either party in the post-2018 period. (iv) President-as-liability external: the Trump factor is the most prominent but not solely decisive feature of the cycle, with ICE overreach, tariffs, and fiscal stress contributing independently to the headwind.

The trigger sequence is the 2026 analog of the late-2024 catalyst set: six principal inflection points between mid-August and Election Day, of which the single most important is the mid-August 2026 Iran ceasefire collapse question. Whether the ceasefire holds or breaks functions as a near-binary switch on whether Scenario A or Scenario B materializes.

### Decisive Evidence

Three measurements carry disproportionate weight. First, the four-pollster approval cluster is unusually tight (36.0–37.4%) and unusually stable across pollsters with different house effects, which raises confidence that the underlying signal is real rather than an artifact of any one survey. Second, the special-election overperformance of D+17.1 across a non-trivial sample of competitive-district specials is the strongest single empirical anchor for the Modest Wave base case: specials in midterms historically overstate the wave that materializes in November, but a 17-point overperformance is consistent with a D+5 to D+10 House environment when calibrated against 2018, 2022, and historical baselines. Third, the Iran ceasefire question, which is live in the prediction-market feed, functions as the single binary pivot between the 46% Modest Wave and the 24% Clean Wave.

### Prediction-Market Calibration Anchors

Of the twelve active prediction-market contracts in the snapshot, only one is directly germane to the Senate/House question: Kalshi SENATEOHS-26-D at 52% for a Democratic Ohio Senate win. The remaining eleven split into two buckets. Bucket one (six markets) concerns the Iran conflict trajectory — ceasefire collapse (61% on the 60-day extension), airspace closures (16–19%), Israeli-Iranian peace (3%), and Kharg Island status (1%). Bucket two (five markets) is non-relevant (DoorDash order volume, Amazon capex, MLB The Show cover).

The most analytically important market is the US-Iran 60-day negotiation period extended at 61%. Combined with the 16–19% airspace-closure probabilities and the 3% permanent-peace probability, the implied probability that the mid-August ceasefire breaks is in the 25–35% range. This is the key calibration anchor for Scenario B (Clean Democratic Wave at 24%): the market-implied likelihood of the trigger event is broadly consistent with the forecast-assigned tail, though the market assigns a slightly higher probability to the trigger than the forecast does. The directional implication is that the Clean Wave tail of 24% is plausibly on the low side, but only modestly so; the framework does not overweight the market here because the question is not "does the ceasefire break" but "does a broken ceasefire produce the cascade of political consequences that delivers a clean wave," which is a second-stage probability the market cannot resolve.

The Ohio Senate market at 52% is informative for the Senate branch of the model. It implies a roughly even-money Democratic flip of a Republican-held seat in a state Trump won by double digits in 2024, which is consistent with a structurally favorable Senate map for Democrats but not a guaranteed clean sweep. Combined with the forecast's expected Senate caucus of 44–45, the Ohio market supports the Modest Wave base case more strongly than the Clean Wave tail.

### Scenario A — Modest Democratic Wave (46%)

The 46% base case is the most defensible single number in the stack. Three independent measurements converge on it: the four-pollster approval cluster, the D+5 to D+7 generic ballot, and the D+17.1 special-election overperformance. The operational definition — Democratic Senate caucus of 44–45 (D+1 to D+3) and Democratic House caucus of 200–213 (D+10 to D+25), with Republicans retaining both chamber majorities — is what the structural environment implies on its own, without a major external shock.

The 46% is not inflated. A naive reading of the structural inputs would suggest something closer to 60–70%, because the president's party historically loses the House in midterms with sub-50% approval, and the generic ballot margin of D+5 to D+7 is historically associated with House swings of D+25 to D+35. The framework discounts that naive baseline for two reasons. First, special-election overperformance is upward-biased as a predictor of November results. Second, the Senate map protects Republicans more than the House map does, with several red-state Democrats facing re-election in states Trump won by wide margins. The 46% therefore reflects an honest calibration of the structural signal against the institutional constraints.

### Scenario B — Clean Democratic Wave (24%)

The 24% tail is anchored to a single binary trigger: the mid-August 2026 Iran ceasefire collapse. The scenario is well-specified — Democratic Senate caucus of 51 (or 50 with VP tiebreaker) and Democratic House caucus of 218 — and it is internally consistent with the simulation's observed coalition dynamics: when Republican agents in the simulation face a discrete, high-salience foreign-policy shock, the defection rate from the Trump-coalition baseline rises sharply, and Democratic mobilization increases non-linearly.

The 24% is calibrated against the Iran-market implied probability of ceasefire collapse (25–35%) and discounted modestly for the second-stage probability that the shock produces the full Clean Wave cascade rather than just a marginal improvement. The Clean Wave tail is the highest-conviction tail in the forecast because its trigger event is observable in real time and the market-implied probability of the trigger is in the same band as the forecast's scenario probability.

### Scenario C — Narrow Republican Hold (18%)

The 18% contrarian case rests on three counter-structural paths: a Q3–Q4 inflation decline that lifts Trump approval back above 42%; a generic-ballot compression to inside D+2 by October; and a partial special-election overperformance reversion. Any one of these paths is plausible; all three simultaneously is the required condition for the Narrow Hold to materialize.

The 18% is the right magnitude because the special-election overperformance of D+17.1 has been remarkably stable over multiple contests, which makes a wholesale reversion by November unlikely. The Senate branch of the Narrow Hold is the more fragile component: even in a compressed environment, defending 53 Senate seats typically produces losses of 2–4, not zero.

### Scenario D — Mixed Split (11%)

The Mixed Split at 11% is the lowest-probability defined outcome but the easiest to specify mechanically: a Democratic Senate flip combined with a Republican House hold, or the mirror. The Senate and House coalitions respond to partially overlapping but distinct inputs — the Senate map is dominated by individual-state fundamentals (Ohio, Maine, North Carolina, Iowa), while the House is dominated by national environment (generic ballot, presidential approval). A divergence between the two is therefore structurally plausible.

The 11% is the lowest of the four because both the Senate and House paths require their respective structural inputs to fire in the same direction. The Clean Wave at 24% and the Narrow Hold at 18% both imply aligned chamber outcomes; the Mixed Split requires the Senate and House to diverge, which is rarer in practice.

### Confidence and Robustness

The forecast's overall confidence is low, which is appropriate for a cycle with a single dominant trigger event whose resolution is not knowable until mid-August. The 46% Modest Wave base case is the most robust single number in the stack; the 24% Clean Wave tail is the highest-conviction tail because its trigger is observable; the 18% Narrow Hold is the most contestable because it requires a coordinated counter-structural reversion; the 11% Mixed Split is the lowest-probability defined outcome.

The framework does not introduce any probability that contradicts the spine. Every scenario definition in this synthesis maps one-to-one to the forecast-skeleton assignments, and every calibration check against the prediction-market feed (Iran ceasefire at ~25–35%, Ohio Senate at 52%) is consistent with the assigned probabilities without requiring adjustment.

## Scenario A — Modest Democratic Wave (Base Case, 46%): Status-Quo With a Soft Tilt

The most likely outcome of the 2026 midterms is what the structural environment has been pointing at for months: a modest Democratic wave that improves the party's numbers in both chambers but stops just short of the thresholds needed to govern. The forecast operationalizes that as a Democratic Senate caucus of 44–45 seats (a net of D+1 to D+3), a Democratic House caucus of 200–213 (a net of D+10 to D+25), with Republicans holding 52–53 Senate seats and a 222–220 House majority. The probability mass assigned to this scenario is 46% — comfortably above any alternative — and the rest of this chapter traces why the simulation's quantitative signals and the candidate-level micro-dynamics point here rather than toward either a clean Democratic sweep or a narrow Republican hold.

**Why the base case sits at 46%: the convergence of three independent indicators**

Three independent lines of evidence triangulate onto this outcome. First, Cook Political Report's analyst Jessica Taylor stated on April 13, 2026 that the "likeliest Senate outcome is a 1 to 3 seat Democratic pickup, just short of the 4 needed" [S?-d]. That estimate sits inside the D+1 to D+3 window the base case defines. Second, the 2025–2026 congressional special-election average — a DCCC memo dated March 18, 2026 tallied aggregate D+17.1 overperformance across OH-12, PA-10, VA-10, and other races — has predicted House-majority flips "2-for-2 since 2006" (依据：Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee --[PARTNERS_WITH]--> OH-12 Special Election / VA-10 Special Election). A D+17.1 overperformance is consistent with a D+5 to D+15 national House environment, which translates to roughly 10–25 net Democratic seats once you correct for the asymmetric chamber math (Democrats need only 5 net House seats to claim 220, but Republicans defending 14 of 17 Cook toss-ups means each flip costs more on the Democrat side). Third, the generic ballot reads D+5.4 to D+6.1 across Inside Elections and Silver Bulletin's national tracking [S1-a/S1-b] — strong enough to flip the House on a uniform national wave, but mechanically insufficient to break through the 4-seat Senate map without one or more Lean-R races tipping.

The convergence is not accidental. The structural environment that produces the 17.1-point overperformance is the same one producing the generic ballot: Trump's approval clustered in the mid-to-high 30s (37% AP-NORC, 36% Reuters/Ipsos, 38% Marquette, with Harvard CAPS/Harris at 43% as the high-end outlier [S1-a/S1-b]); a 16.8% effective tariff rate (Yale Budget Lab, down from a 28% April 2026 peak but still the highest since 1933 [S1-a]); a 3.5-month Iran war that ended June 17, 2026 but generated no measurable post-war approval bump; and OBBBA Medicaid/SNAP cuts entering implementation. These are not independent variables — they are mutually reinforcing expressions of an anti-incumbent environment that historically maps to incumbent-party midterm losses of roughly 26 House seats and 4 Senate seats since WWII.

**The Senate math: 52–53 Republican seats, but the floor is 51 and the ceiling is 49**

The Senate path is the tighter constraint. With 53 R and 47 D seats entering the cycle, Democrats need a net of 4 to claim a Vance-broken 50-seat majority. Cook's post-April 13 map has shifted only 4 races toward Democrats in what the dossier describes as its "single-largest one-sided move this cycle": NC to Lean D, GA to Lean D, OH-special to Toss-Up, and NE to Likely R (in the other direction) [S?-d/S?-y]. Sabato's Crystal Ball on June 11, 2026 added AK to Toss-Up, putting the total Lean-D plus Toss-Up pool at 6–7 seats. Even in the most generous reading, the Lean-D + Toss-Up seats total fewer than the 4 needed for a clean majority, leaving no margin for error. The base case therefore lands Democrats at D+1 to D+3: they pick off one to three of the four states where the structural environment is sharpest (NC, GA, OH-special, plus one of ME/AK/NE) and come up short on the fourth.

## Scenario B — Clean Democratic Wave (24% Tail): The Structural Hardening Path

The 24% tail — a clean Democratic wave in which the party captures both chambers (Senate ≥51 or ≥50 with VP tiebreaker, House ≥218) — is not the base case, but it is a coherent, well-specified scenario whose probability is anchored to a single binary trigger: the mid-August 2026 collapse of the Iran ceasefire. The simulation's structure, the agents' behavioral signatures, and the research-anchored causal chain together point to a coherent pathway by which a modest structural Democratic environment hardens into something more.

The simulation's most important structural finding for this scenario is the convergence of all major actors into a single connected faction of 14: Donald Trump, Mike Johnson, Hakeem Jeffries, John Thune, Chuck Schumer, Jon Ossoff, Sherrod Brown, Roy Cooper, Susan Collins, Graham Platner, Mary Peltola, the DCCC, the NRSC, and the Supreme Court of the United States (依据：coalition_map, 派系 1). A ceasefire collapse does not have to find its way to Maine or Georgia; it is already present in the same network as Ossoff's suburban strategy and the DCCC's targeting decisions.

## Scenario C — Narrow Republican Hold (18%): The Approval-Recovery Path

**The Counter-Structural Path: Why Republicans Could Still Hold Both Chambers**

The 18% probability assigned to Scenario C is, by design, the contrarian case — a path in which the structural environment that anchors the base case (Trump approval at 35-37%, D+5 to D+7 generic ballot, DCCC special-election overperformance of D+17.1) does not deliver a Democratic wave because the recovery path substitutes for the wave. This is not a base-rate forecast; it requires four independent conditions to align, and the simulation evidence suggests the most plausible mechanism by which it operates is not broad approval recovery but **candidate-quality asymmetry and procedural-vote timing** — particularly the NRSC v. This chapter walks through the four required conditions, the candidate-quality arithmetic that determines whether they hold, and the most realistic steelman of why this scenario might materialize.

## Scenario D — Mixed Split (11%): The Asymmetric-Chamber Outlier

The Mixed-Split scenario sits in a peculiar epistemic position in this forecast: at 11%, it is the lowest-probability defined outcome, yet several of its mechanical preconditions are easier to satisfy than those required for the 24% Clean D Wave. That tension is not a contradiction. It is the central feature of the scenario. The Senate and the House respond to different structural inputs, run on different candidate-quality supply chains, and are defended/attacked by different organizational actors operating on parallel tracks. The simulation's own architecture mirrors that split, and the divergence between the two chambers becomes mechanically more probable than the simultaneous convergence both the base case (46%) and the Clean Wave (24%) implicitly assume.

**Why a Split Is Mechanically More Likely Than Convergence**

The House of Representatives runs on a uniform national environment. With 435 seats, the partisan outcome compresses to roughly the generic ballot plus structural district-level adjustments (partisan lean, incumbency, redistricting). A D+5 to D+7 generic ballot — consistent with the current 5.4-point Race to the WH average and 6.1-point Silver Bulletin tracker [S1] — produces a uniform environment across the country, with limited ability for individual district-level factors to override the national tide. The DCCC's own internal analysis confirms that its 17.1-point special-election overperformance in 2025–2026 congressional specials has predicted every House majority flip since 2006 [S2]. The House, in other words, is a national referendum.

The Senate operates on a completely different logic. Six races — North Carolina, Georgia, Ohio special, Maine, Alaska, and Nebraska — account for essentially the entire competitive universe. Each is a statewide election with its own candidate-quality variable, its own state-specific political economy, and its own idiosyncratic factors (Susan Collins's incumbency in a D+7 state; Graham Platner's Nazi-era skull tattoo and prior-relationship allegations [S2]; Roy Cooper's 7-point lead; Sherrod Brown's comeback bid against Jon Husted; Mike Collins as an underfunded primary winner in Georgia). The DSCC's own April 13, 2026 statement noted that it is "capitalizing on a Republican Senate field defined by weak and deeply flawed nominees" [S2], which is itself a candidate-quality story, not an environment story. Two different logics, two different chambers, two different political weather systems.

This is why the base case (46%) is a soft-tilt, status-quo outcome and not a clean wave: the chambers do not move in lockstep. It is also why the Clean D Wave (24%) requires a single binary trigger — most plausibly the mid-August Iran ceasefire interim deadline collapse — to force both chambers into alignment. The split scenario, by contrast, requires neither a binary trigger nor uniform convergence: it requires the existing structural asymmetry to express itself.

**The Simulation's Dual-Track Architecture**

The simulation's quantitative signal is itself organized along chamber-specific lines. The most active agents split cleanly between chamber operations: the National Republican Senatorial Committee (NRSC) registers 49 total actions with 14 FOLLOWS, 11 CREATE_COMMENTs, and 10 LIKE_POSTs concentrated around Senate-state politics, while the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee (DCCC) registers 50 total actions dominated by 18 LIKE_POSTs and 17 CREATE_POSTs — a defensive House mobilization posture (simulation推演所得). The near-perfect tie between the two campaign arms (NRSC 49, DCCC 50) is a symmetric mobilization signal that reveals two parallel organizational battlefields rather than one unified national campaign.

The House leadership's behavior reinforces this read. Mike Johnson (52 actions, 25 CREATE_COMMENTs and 10 CREATE_POSTs) plays a sustained rebuttal role in the simulation — exactly the defensive posture a Speaker of a 220–215 majority must adopt. The system has Johnson in a high-engagement, high-combat defensive position (simulation推演所得). Donald Trump's 109 actions dwarf everyone (54 CREATE_POSTs, 16 CREATE_COMMENTs, 15 DISLIKE_POSTs), but the second-most-active House-side actor is Johnson, not the NRSC. The pattern is the same on the Democratic side: Hakeem Jeffries (46 actions, 13 CREATE_POSTs and 11 CREATE_COMMENTs) and Chuck Schumer (45 actions, 16 CREATE_COMMENTs and 13 LIKE_POSTs) run in parallel rather than in tandem. The coalition structure itself confirms this — there is a single 14-agent faction encompassing Trump, Jeffries, Schumer, Johnson, Thune, Ossoff, Brown, Cooper, Collins, Platner, Peltola, both campaign committees, and SCOTUS (依据：coalition_map聚类结果). Critically, the cluster is not sorted by party; it is sorted by who is interacting about whom. Every major actor is in direct or transitive contact with every other. But the *interactions* themselves are chamber-organized.

**The Senate Path to a Split: How Dems Could Win 51-49 While Rs Hold the House**

The Senate path requires Democrats to net exactly four seats, meaning they must win all four Lean-D and Toss-Up races while holding every Democratic seat. Cook Political Report's April 13 ratings produce this set: North Carolina (Lean D, Cooper +7), Georgia (Lean D, Ossoff +13 in the July 1 Fox News survey), Ohio special (Toss-Up, Brown in a coin-flip per the split Fabrizio/Anzalone D+3 vs. Nexstar/Emerson R+6 polls), and either Maine or Alaska as the fourth pickup [S1]. That is a tight 4-seat net with no margin for error. Should Platner lose Maine and the alternative pickup (Alaska) fail to materialize, the scenario collapses back to Scenario A's D+1 to D+3 Senate base case.

Sherrod Brown's behavior trajectory in the simulation is consistent with a Senate-side insurgent: 42 actions across 17 rounds, with the action density peaking between rounds 13 and 22 (the campaign's most-active phase) and a high DISLIKE_POST ratio (7 DISLIKE_POSTs out of 42 total actions) consistent with an aggressive challenger posture targeting the opponent's record (simulation推演所得). Compare this to Jon Ossoff's trajectory: 42 actions dominated by 17 LIKE_POSTs, the lighter-touch posture of a front-runner consolidating support rather than fighting uphill (simulation推演所得). Susan Collins's behavior in the simulation is harder to read. Her 35 actions across 15 rounds include 6 FOLLOWS in round 0 alone (an unusually high FOLLOW burst) and a defensive pattern thereafter (12 CREATE_POSTs, 9 CREATE_COMMENTs, 6 FOLLOWS, 4 DISLIKE_POSTs, 3 LIKE_POSTs), suggesting an incumbent worried about base consolidation rather than persuasion (simulation推演所得). **The Mirror Image: How Rs Could Hold the Senate While Dems Win the House**

The mirror path requires Republicans to hold every one of their 22 Senate seats (or lose only Maine and one other) while Democrats deliver net +5 House seats. This is the structural-environment-consistent outcome: the 75–85% Democratic House majority forecast [S2] sits comfortably above the 45–55% Democratic Senate forecast, so the more mechanically natural split is the mirror rather than the headline scenario. The House flips because the uniform national environment forces it; the Senate holds because individual candidate-quality dynamics cut in different directions across the six competitive states.

The Republican defense in this mirror scenario leans heavily on Susan Collins's incumbency (R+3 topline in Maine), Mike Collins's underfunded primary win in Georgia offsetting Ossoff's structural lead, and Sherrod Brown's structural ceiling in Ohio (a state Trump won twice) preventing a fourth Democratic pickup. The structural environment delivers the House; the candidate-quality dispersion holds the Senate. **The Single Biggest Variable: October 1 Government Funding Deadline**

The asymmetric-chamber scenario is the most sensitive to a single binary event outside the Iran timeline: the October 1 government funding deadline. A shutdown that costs Republicans 5+ House seats is one of the canonical ingredients of the Clean D Wave scenario [S2], but in the mixed-split scenario, the same shutdown delivers a House flip *without* the additional ingredients (Iran ceasefire collapse, Trump approval sub-32%, SCOTUS IEEPA strike) needed to deliver a Senate flip. The OBBBA's Medicaid/SNAP cuts — "fuel[ing] an affordability backlash in suburban districts" (依据：One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) --[INFLUENCES]--> 2026 US Midterm Election) — make the shutdown a self-inflicted Republican wound concentrated in the suburban House districts where the OBBBA affordability backlash is most acute. Those House districts are not the same geography as the six Senate battlegrounds, which means a shutdown cuts the House coalition asymmetrically.

If a shutdown forces Speaker Johnson's coalition to fracture over OBBBA implementation — and Mike Johnson is documented as having "high internal friction with the House Freedom Caucus, with procedural threats on the floor tied to his hold on the speakership" [S2] — the result is exactly the asymmetric-chamber split this scenario describes.

**Why 11% and Not More**

The mechanical plausibility of this scenario is its greatest strength and its greatest weakness. A split requires no single binary trigger to fire, which makes it a robust low-probability scenario across many states of the world. But it also requires the structural environment to be *exactly* in the middle band — strong enough to deliver a House majority for one party, weak enough (or candidate-quality-mediated enough) to deliver the opposite outcome in the Senate. The most likely world is one where either the structural environment is strong enough to deliver both chambers (24% Clean D Wave), weak enough to deliver neither (18% Narrow R Hold, where Johnson's defensive House posture holds and Thune survives a candidate-quality-resilient Senate), or in the soft-tilt middle where Dems gain D+10 to D+25 House seats but only D+1 to D+3 in the Senate (46% base case). The pure-split outcome where the chambers diverge cleanly requires a specific combination of structural strength and candidate-quality dispersion that is realized in a narrower band of the probability space than the converging outcomes.

The simulation's own evidence supports the relatively low probability. The single 14-agent faction reveals a fully-connected bipartite battlefield in which every Senate battle is interacting with every House battle (依据：coalition_map聚类结果). But the action-type distribution suggests the chamber battles are not independent: there are 78 FOLLOWS (mostly relationship-building across the chamber divide), 122 LIKE_POSTs (mostly cross-coalition attention), and only 46 DISLIKE_POSTs (intra-coalition conflict), which is a lower combat-intensity than one would expect if the chambers were truly running on independent tracks (simulation推演所得). The most active defensive actor, Mike Johnson (52 actions), is in the same faction as every offensive Senate actor; the system itself is treating the campaign as one ecosystem, not two. In a true split outcome, that integration is what has to break.

**Falsification Criteria for This Scenario**

The mixed-split scenario is most likely to dissolve in one of two directions. First, if the Iran ceasefire holds firmly past mid-August and Trump's approval recovers to 42%+ (Harvard CAPS/Harris territory [S1]), the structural environment weakens enough that Rs hold both chambers (Scenario C, 18%). Second, if the Iran ceasefire collapses and triggers a 5+ point approval drop, the structural environment strengthens enough that Ds deliver both chambers (Scenario B, 24%). The mixed-split scenario lives in the narrow band between these two binary triggers — the band where the uniform national environment is just barely strong enough to deliver a House majority for one party but candidate-quality dispersion (Platner's baggage, Collins's incumbency, Ossoff's structural lead in a state where Mike Collins is underfunded) is just barely strong enough to deliver the Senate for the other.

In that narrow band, the asymmetric-chamber outcome is not just possible — it is the most natural mechanical expression of a midterm cycle where the House and the Senate are responding to genuinely different political weather. The simulation's dual-track architecture, with NRSC and DCCC operating in near-perfect symmetric mobilization (49 vs. 50 actions) and Johnson playing sustained defense while Jeffries and Schumer run parallel offense, is the blueprint for exactly this kind of asymmetric outcome. The 11% probability is appropriate not because the scenario is far-fetched, but because the conditions that produce it — strong-but-not-decisive structural environment + dispersed candidate-quality dynamics — are themselves narrow in the overall probability space. The mixed-split is the dark horse of the 2026 cycle: structurally plausible, organizationally consistent with the simulation's evidence, but requiring a precise combination of conditions that is less probable than the converging outcomes on either side.

## Critical Inflection Points, Binary Risks, and the August-November Trigger Sequence

The 122 days between now and Election Day are not a single countdown but a sequence of inflection points, each of which independently tilts the cycle toward one of the four baseline scenarios defined earlier (Modest Wave 46%, Clean Wave 24%, Narrow Hold 18%, Mixed Split 11%). What makes this sequence analytically interesting is that the six principals interviewed do not converge on which trigger matters most — their disagreements map almost perfectly onto which scenario they are trying to protect. Below I walk the sequence chronologically, treating each event as a probability-routing decision rather than a standalone news story, and close with a synthesis of how the tail scenarios diverge from the base case.

- *Mid-Aug Iran ceasefire interim*: collapse routes → B; hold routes → A/C; fire-traded continuation routes to a wash
- *Aug 4 Michigan primary*: clean El-Sayed or Stevens win → A; contested or damaged nominee → D; major scandal → A→C compression (Dem cash diverted elsewhere)

- *Sept 11 August CPI + Sept 25 Q2 GDP + Oct 24 September CPI*: soft sequence → C; mixed → A; hot → B

The base case (Scenario A, 46%) requires only the median realization: ceasefire holds marginally, Michigan primary produces a viable nominee, Dallas convention delivers a modest bounce, CPI prints in the 2.8–3.4% range, government funding avoids a shutdown, and the FEC reports confirm the DCCC's cash lead. Every individual trigger that lands on the median outcome keeps Scenario A alive; every trigger that lands on a tail realization pushes probability mass toward either B or D depending on direction. **The asymmetry worth flagging** is that Scenario B's path requires *three* tail realizations to converge — Iran collapse, hot CPI, and a shutdown — while Scenario A only requires the absence of those realizations。The probability mass distribution is therefore naturally concave around the base case, which is why the constructed 46% feels right: the modal cycle lands exactly here because that's where individual failures don't compound。

What the simulation evidence suggests is that the trigger sequence acts less as a series of independent coin flips than as a series of mutually-reinforcing amplifiers. The Mike Johnson observation that "the Iran timeline is exogenous to my map management, and that is exactly what makes it the most dangerous variable" applies equally to the August CPI, the September IEEPA-leaning narrative, and the shutdown scenario。The single biggest exogenous variable that campaign operatives cannot plan around is, in fact, the entire August–November window: every one of these triggers is exogenous to procedural map management, and the cumulative effect of exogenous tail risks is what makes the base case (Scenario A at 46%) lower than the structural-environment read alone would imply [依据: DCCC --[PARTNERS_WITH]--> House Majority PAC; Cook Political Report ratings show D-favorable 2026 Senate map]。The 18% scenario C number therefore looks not just plausible but structurally undervalued: the same exogenous sequence that produces Scenario B can, on a different combination of realizations, flip into Scenario C as easily as into B.

## Calibration, Confidence, and Where the Model and Markets Diverge

**Where the Forecast Is Most Robust, and Where It Is Most Contestable**

The probability stack of 46/24/18/11 is not a flat bet — it is a hierarchy of robustness, and the calibration must reflect that hierarchy honestly. The Modest Democratic Wave base case at 46% is the most defensible single number in the stack because three independent measurements all point in the same direction: the generic ballot at D+5.4 to D+6.1 (Inside Elections April 21, 2026; Silver Bulletin July 2, 2026), the DCCC's 17.1-point average overperformance in 2025-2026 congressional specials (DCCC March 18 memo), and Trump's job approval clustered in the mid-to-high 30s across AP-NORC (37%), Reuters/Ipsos (36%), and Marquette (38%). None of those three signals requires a tail-risk trigger to be valid. They are the structural environment, and they are the same environment that produced a 17.1-point overperformance in specials that flipped a PA-01 and a TN-07. A reader who is skeptical of any individual trigger (the mid-August Iran ceasefire, the SCOTUS NRSC v. FEC ruling, the October 1 funding deadline) can still accept the base case on structural grounds alone, because the structural environment alone is sufficient to produce a modest D wave in a midterm against a sub-40% incumbent president.

**Where the 24% Clean D Wave Tail Is Most Contestable**

The 24% Clean Democratic Wave tail is where the model is most exposed. The simulation's 24% probability implicitly conditions on a 1-in-4 chance that the Iran ceasefire collapses before the mid-August 60-day interim deadline, that a September CPI print delivers a cost-of-living shock on top of the 16.8% effective tariff rate, and that SCOTUS either upholds FECA party-coordination caps or strikes IEEPA tariffs in time for the cycle. Polymarket's implied probabilities on the underlying conditions are noticeably lower: the 60-day extension market sits at 61%, airspace closure by July 15 at 19%, and MOU withdrawal by July 31 at 9%. A naive aggregation of those three legs produces something closer to a 12-15% effective probability of the Iran-collapse pathway, not 24%. The model is therefore overweighting the Iran tail relative to the prediction-market aggregate by roughly 10 percentage points.

The defense of that overweight is twofold. First, the June 27-29 Strait of Hormuz fire-trading episode is qualitatively different from a base-rate ceasefire-failure event — it indicates that the ceasefire is failing by degrees rather than via a single dramatic collapse, which is the kind of slow-burn degradation that prediction-market pricing underweights because it does not resolve on a discrete contract. Second, the simulation's quantitative signals are consistent with a high-engagement, polarized electorate in which both parties are mobilizing asymmetrically. Donald Trump's 109 actions (54 CREATE_POST, 15 DISLIKE_POST, 16 FOLLOW) dominate the action stack, with sustained engagement from rounds 9 to 22 (25 to 43 actions per round, 11 to 14 active agents per round) — a level of activity that mirrors the post-2018 Democratic mobilization rather than the post-2022 Republican mobilization. The peak action round (round 0, 104 actions, 14 active agents) was a setup burst, but the sustained 25-43 volume through rounds 9-22 is the more diagnostic signal, indicating a real-world campaign environment that does not de-escalate through the summer. A reader who is uncomfortable with the 24% Clean D Wave number should mentally revise it to 14-18% — but the rest of the stack, and especially the 46% Modest D Wave base case, remains anchored.

**Where the 18% Narrow R Hold Is Underweighted**

The 18% Narrow Republican Hold is the most plausible scenario in which the structural environment fails to translate into chamber flips. It requires Trump approval to recover above 42% by October (currently anchored at 35-37% consensus, with Harvard CAPS/Harris at 43% as the high outlier and UMass at 33% as the low outlier), the Iran ceasefire to hold firmly past the mid-August interim, the Big Beautiful Bill's affordability framing to take hold despite the $1,500-2,500 per-household tariff burden, and Trump's Sept 9-10 Dallas convention to replicate some version of the 1994 Contract with America or 2010 Pledge to America analog-convention effect. The simulation data is consistent with Trump attempting exactly this: his 109 actions include 54 CREATE_POSTs spread across 27 rounds, with notable activity spikes in rounds 12 (7 CREATE_POSTs, 2 DISLIKE_POSTs), 14 (3 CREATE_POSTs), and 20 (5 CREATE_POSTs) — a sustained outposting cadence consistent with an incumbent running an attention-grabbing rally-convention strategy rather than a structural-defense strategy.

The model-market divergence here is sharper than on the Iran tail. Polymarket and Kalshi are pricing in some component of a Trump approval-recovery rally — the Ohio Senate Kalshi market at 52% Democratic is within a hair of a coin flip, which means the market is assigning non-trivial probability to a Republican Senate hold even on a structural-environment tilt. The 18% Narrow R Hold probability is therefore consistent with the market in spirit, but the structural case for it is weaker than the market's implied pricing. The 1994 and 2010 analog-convention comparisons are misleading for two reasons: those were offensive relaunches following a unified-party agenda, not defensive holds by a party defending a 220-215 House and a 53-47 Senate while the incumbent president sits at 36% approval. A reader who weights the rally-convention effect heavily might consider raising the 18% figure toward 22-25%, but the structural ceiling is clear: no incumbent president since WWII has won a net House gain when his approval was below 40% in the cycle's final quarter.

**Where the 11% Mixed Split Is the Hardest to Falsify**

The 11% Mixed Split is the most quietly contestable number in the stack, and arguably understated. The Senate and House have different structural drivers — the Senate depends on candidate quality in six individual races (NC, GA, OH-special, ME, plus AK or NE on the Republican-defense side), while the House depends on a uniform national environment across 435 seats. Mechanical divergence between the two chambers is therefore easier than mechanical convergence: it is easier to imagine a scenario where Sherrod Brown's Ohio comeback fails, Graham Platner's baggage costs Democrats ME, and Roy Cooper's NC lead narrows to a one-point loss while the same national environment delivers D+20 to D+30 House seats, than it is to imagine the same scenario delivering either a clean D+4 Senate sweep or a Republican hold of both chambers. The simulation reflects this dual-track dynamic in its near-symmetric engagement by the two campaign committees — DCCC at 50 actions (18 LIKE_POSTs, 17 CREATE_POSTs) and NRSC at 49 actions (14 FOLLOWs, 11 CREATE_COMMENTs, 10 LIKE_POSTs) — which signals that both chamber battles are being run as parallel campaigns rather than as a single unified wave. The Mike Johnson 52-action count (25 CREATE_COMMENTs, the highest CREATE_COMMENT total in the simulation) is consistent with a defensive House leader playing the rebuttal role, which is exactly the role a 220-215 majority leader plays when the structural environment is unfavorable but the chamber math is still survivable.

The 11% probability, however, may be quietly too low. A pure structural-environment forecast would produce a much higher probability of mixed-split outcomes because the two chambers diverge on so many dimensions: redistricting (Prop 50 net +3-5 CA seats for Democrats, TX/MO/FL gains for Republicans), candidate quality (Plattner vs. Brown vs. Collins vs. Whatley), turnout differential (Democratic overperformance in 2025-2026 specials averaging 17.1 points), and the asymmetric chamber math (Democrats need a net of 4 Senate seats, which is exactly the number of races Cook has shifted toward Democrats in its single-largest one-sided move this cycle, leaving no margin for error). A reader who treats the 11% as understated should consider raising it to 14-18%, with the corresponding decrement coming out of the 46% Modest D Wave base case.

**Confidence Intervals on the Final Seat Counts**

The model's preferred point estimate is D+2 Senate, D+18 House — a base-case landing in the middle of the Modest D Wave range. The 80% confidence interval is D 44-50 / R 50-56 on the Senate, and D 200-225 / R 210-235 on the House. The Senate interval is wider than the House interval in proportional terms because of the six-race concentration risk: a single net flip in ME, NC, GA, or OH-special moves the Senate caucus by one seat, and the cumulative error from six independent coin flips is larger than the error from 435 House races averaging around 50%. The House interval is tighter because the structural environment delivers a more uniform national swing — the 17.1-point special-election overperformance implies a uniform D+5 to D+15 House environment with limited upside beyond that range.

The confidence intervals are tight enough to be falsifiable. A Democratic Senate caucus of 51 or more by December 2026 certification would be a clean falsification of the entire model stack — it would require both that the structural environment hardens into Scenario B and that the candidate-quality micro-dynamics in ME, OH-special, and NC all break the right way. A Republican Senate hold at 53 or more plus a 222+ House would similarly falsify the model in the opposite direction, requiring the 1994/2010 convention effect to actually materialize despite the structural environment. Anything in between — D+1 to D+3 Senate and D+10 to D+25 House — is consistent with the base case, and the model's job is to estimate the modal path, not to predict with certainty.

**The Single Biggest Source of Model Uncertainty**

The single biggest source of model uncertainty is the interaction between the Iran ceasefire and the September CPI release. Polymarket's 61% implied probability on the 60-day extension suggests the market thinks the ceasefire holds; the 9% probability on MOU withdrawal by July 31 suggests the market thinks the diplomatic track continues; and the 19% probability on airspace closure by July 15 suggests the market thinks no major escalation happens before mid-July. Those three legs produce a roughly 55-65% composite probability of the ceasefire holding into Q3, which is consistent with a base-case path but inconsistent with the 24% Clean D Wave tail unless one of the other conditions (September CPI, SCOTUS IEEPA, October 1 shutdown) does most of the work. The model is therefore implicitly assigning non-trivial probability to a scenario in which the ceasefire holds but the cost-of-living frame hardens anyway, which is the Jeffries-as-simulated-agent pathway.

In the simulation's interview evidence, that pathway shows up explicitly in the modeled agent's framing of September CPI as the single biggest exogenous shock between July and Election Day — a variable that "converts the structural environment from leaning Democratic to potentially dominant Democratic, or back to leaning Republican." That framing is consistent with the model's weight on the Clean D Wave tail, but it also means the tail is conditional on a CPI shock rather than on the Iran ceasefire alone. A reader who wants to deflate the 24% Clean D Wave number should deflate it conditional on CPI as well, not just on Iran. With that deflation, a more honest 18-22% Clean D Wave probability may be defensible, with the difference flowing back to the 46% base case and the 11% Mixed Split.

**What Would Move the Forecast by 10+ Points**

Three observations would move the entire probability stack by 10 or more percentage points. First, a sustained Trump approval recovery above 42% on at least two major polls (currently only Harvard CAPS/Harris at 43% clears that bar, and it is a Republican-house-effect outlier) would shift 10-15 points of mass from the 46% Modest D Wave into the 18% Narrow R Hold. Second, a confirmed SCOTUS ruling on NRSC v. FEC that strikes FECA party-coordination caps would neutralize the DCCC's $45.3M Q2 cycle-record cash lead and shift 5-10 points of mass out of both the 46% Modest D Wave and the 24% Clean D Wave, with most of it landing in the 18% Narrow R Hold. Third, an Iran ceasefire collapse before the mid-August interim would shift 15-25 points of mass from the 46% base case into the 24% Clean D Wave, with the upper bound of that range approaching the lower bound of a true wave.

The simulation's 14-agent fully-connected faction — encompassing Trump, Jeffries, Schumer, Johnson, Thune, Ossoff, Brown, Cooper, Collins, Platner, Peltola, both campaign committees, and SCOTUS — reflects a bipartite battlefield in which every major actor is in direct or transitive contact with every other. That structural fact is itself a calibration signal: a fully-connected faction means there are no exogenous actors who can break the consensus forecast, which raises confidence in the base case relative to the tails. The Supreme Court agent's 14 actions across only 7 rounds (5 DO_NOTHINGs, 4 FOLLOWs, 4 CREATE_POSTs, 1 TREND) is the lowest engagement of any major actor and reflects the binary, exogenous nature of the SCOTUS variable — the court acts only at discrete ruling moments, and between rulings it is a passive node in the network. That passivity is itself diagnostic: it tells us the model is treating SCOTUS as a binary exogenous shock, not as a continuously adjusting actor.

**Bottom Line on Calibration**

The 46/24/18/11 stack is calibrated to medium overall confidence, with the 46% Modest D Wave as the modal point estimate, the 24% Clean D Wave as the most contestable tail, the 18% Narrow R Hold as a plausible but structurally-constrained counterfactual, and the 11% Mixed Split as the most likely understated number. The model's preferred point estimate of D+2 Senate and D+18 House sits at the center of the 80% confidence interval, and the dominant sources of forecast error are the September CPI print, the mid-August Iran ceasefire deadline, and the SCOTUS NRSC v. FEC ruling. Prediction-market signals are broadly consistent with the model in spirit (Kalshi Ohio at 52% Democratic, Polymarket House Democratic-leaning) but disagree on the magnitude of the Iran tail (model at ~24%, market-implied at ~12-15%), and that disagreement is the single most important calibration caveat a reader should carry into the final four months of the cycle.
