# When Two Tsunamis Meet: Modern Mercantilism × Artificial Intelligence, 2026–2036

### A Forecast Dossier

---

## Executive Summary

Two structural forces are colliding in real time. **Modern Mercantilism** — the return of the state as an active director of trade, industrial capacity, and strategic technology — is rewriting the rules of the global economy through tariffs, export controls, sovereign compute, and direct state ownership of strategic firms. **Artificial Intelligence** is simultaneously the largest capital-formation event of the decade and the terrain on which great-power competition is now fought. These forces are not sequential. They are mutually amplifying: AI has become the *object* of mercantilist policy (chips, compute, energy, data), while mercantilism has become the *constraint* that shapes where and how fast AI can be built.

This dossier defends a single load-bearing thesis and organizes ~40 gathered sources — including the **February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down IEEPA tariffs**, the **IEA's Energy & AI** projections, **MIT NANDA's "95% of GenAI pilots fail"** finding, **Anthropic's Economic Index (March 2026)**, and hard capital figures on hyperscaler capex, sovereign-AI commitments, and semiconductor reshoring — into a coherent framework, ten-plus calibrated binary forecasts, and a full analytical appendix.

The central finding is uncomfortable for both bulls and bears: the **transformation is real and the near-term macro payoff is small**. Tariffs are durable even when illegal. AI adoption is wide but shallow. Both great powers are fiscally constrained. And the same bond market is being pulled apart by government deficits and AI capex simultaneously.

## PART 0 — THE INSIGHT CONTRACT

### THE THESIS (load-bearing, falsifiable)

> **Over 2026–2030, Modern Mercantilism and AI will co-produce a world of *durable elevated tariffs, sovereign-directed compute buildout, and wide-but-shallow AI adoption* — meaning the effective US tariff rate stays above 10%, AI capex keeps growing, but measured aggregate productivity gains remain small (sub-1pp/yr TFP) as the collision concentrates gains in capital, energy-rich states, and high-tenure adopters while the financing strain surfaces in the bond market before it surfaces in output.**

This is falsifiable. The thesis **breaks** if any of the following occur before 2030: the US effective tariff rate falls durably below 10%; measured US labor-productivity growth sustainably exceeds ~2.5%/yr attributable to AI; or AI capex collapses (a >40% year-over-year decline in hyperscaler capex) without a rate/fiscal shock triggering it.

### The Non-Obvious Core

The consensus splits into two camps — the **AI-productivity-boom camp** (Goldman: ~1.5pp/yr TFP uplift) and the **AI-bubble camp** (Burry's ~$1.1B in puts; MIT's 95%-failure finding). **Both are partly wrong about timing.** The load-bearing insight is that *these are not contradictory*: AI can be simultaneously **real, transformative, over-financed in the short run, and slow to show up in TFP** — exactly as electricity was. The binding variable in 2026–2030 is not "does AI work" but "who can finance and *power* it," and that question is answered by mercantilist policy and the bond market, not by model capability.

## PART 2 — FRAMEWORK & HOLISTIC SYNTHESIS

### The Collision Map

The interaction of Modern Mercantilism and AI is best understood through **five structural facts** established in the research, each of which becomes a lever the two forces pull on together.

**1. Tariffs are durable even when illegal.** On **February 20, 2026**, the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 (Roberts) in *Learning Resources v. Trump / Trump v. V.O.S. Selections* that **IEEPA does not authorize tariffs**, striking down the "Liberation Day" reciprocal tariffs and border/fentanyl tariffs. Tax Foundation estimated **>$160B had been collected illegally** and that the IEEPA regime would have raised **$1.4T over 2026–2034**. Yet within days the administration pivoted to **Section 122 (10% global stopgap) + Section 232/301** authorities, and by **April 2026 the effective US tariff rate settled just above 11%** — Section 122 accounting for ~26% of it. This is the single most important calibration fact in the dossier: *the tools changed but the wall stayed up* (F1, F2). The Smoot-Hawley base rate reinforces this — the 1930 tariff was not meaningfully unwound until the RTAA (1934) and the postwar GATT order. **Entrenched tariff regimes persist for years-to-decades and require deliberate political reversal, not automatic decay.**

**2. AI is the object of mercantilist policy.** Chips, compute, energy, and data are now treated as strategic national assets. The US uses **export controls** on advanced chips to China; China retaliates with **rare-earth and gallium/germanium controls** (tightened Oct 9, 2025; suspended to the US until Nov 27, 2026 under a Trump-Xi truce). Japan moved from grants to **direct state equity + a golden-share veto** in Rapidus (June 2026, ¥150B injection). Saudi Arabia's **HUMAIN** (PIF-owned, launched May 13, 2025) is converting sovereign capital, land, and power into **600,000 NVIDIA GPUs, 14 GW of power, and a 6.6 GW compute pipeline by 2034**. This is compute as statecraft (F11).

**3. The buildout is enormous — and financed in the same bond market as the government.** Big-4 hyperscaler capex is projected at **~$700–725B in 2026, up ~77% from ~$410B in 2025** (F3). But the financing channel is the load-bearing risk: **AI-linked bond issuance hit a record ~$220B in 2025**; the big-5 issued **$121B in corporate bonds — over 4× the $28B five-year average**. Simultaneously, the **Treasury is issuing more long-term debt than any time since the financial crisis**, with **net interest hitting $1T in FY2026, doubling to $2.1T by 2036**, and debt/GDP rising **101%→120%** (F12). The circular dynamic is explicit: more Treasury issuance raises yields → raises corporate borrowing costs → makes AI capex more expensive → forces more debt or cash flow. Combined Big-4 free cash flow is projected to fall to **~$4B in Q3 2026**, with **Amazon projecting ~−$17B FCF** for the year. Michael Burry disclosed **~$1.1B in notional puts** against AI names in Q3 2025 (F5).

**4. The near-term productivity payoff is small.** MIT NANDA's *The GenAI Divide* found **95% of enterprise GenAI pilots deliver zero measurable ROI** (F10). Paul David's *The Dynamo and the Computer* (1990) provides the base rate: factory electrification took **~40 years** from dynamo introduction (~1880s) to the productivity surge (~1920s) because factories had to be physically redesigned around the technology. Anthropic's Economic Index (March 2026) reconciles the tension: **~49% of jobs have had ≥25% of their tasks touched by Claude** — wide penetration — but adoption is **uneven and augmentative**, concentrated among high-tenure users and high-income regions (top-20 countries = 48% of per-capita usage). The pattern is **wide, shallow adoption; thin, deep monetization** (F4).

**5. Both great powers are fiscally constrained, and energy is the new binding input.** The US faces the $1T→$2.1T interest trajectory; China's **10 trillion RMB local-debt cleanup (Nov 2024) is falling short**, with hidden LGFV liabilities dwarfing official estimates. Neither has clean fiscal room for an unconstrained AI race. Meanwhile the IEA confirms data-centre electricity rising from **~415 TWh (2024, ~1.5% of global) to ~945 TWh by 2030 (~3%)**, with **AI-accelerated servers growing ~30%/yr** — but staying a *limited* global share (F6). The real constraint is **local grid siting and lead-time**, which advantages the energy-abundant: China is **33% of G20 power generation vs the US's 15%**.

### The Cause→Effect Engine (with second-order effects)

The forecasts hang together through the following mechanisms:

- **CHAIN A (Tariff durability → onshoring → back-loaded supply):** SCOTUS strikes IEEPA → administration substitutes Section 232/301 → effective rate stays >10% (F1/F2) → firms commit to US fabs (TSMC ≥$100B, Japan Rapidus, Korea's $471B supercluster) → **but leading-edge output is a ~2030 story (F9)** → near-term US chip autonomy stays low → continued dependence on Taiwan/Korea → **second-order: Taiwan-risk premium persists and the truce with China becomes strategically necessary**.

- **CHAIN B (AI capex → bond supply → rates → capex constraint):** Hyperscalers borrow ~$220B (F3) → collides with record Treasury issuance → 10Y stays >4% (F12) → cost of AI capital rises → FCF turns negative → **second-order: a self-imposed 18–36 month monetization deadline that AI's slow diffusion (F4/F10) cannot meet → financing stress event (F5)**.

- **CHAIN C (Mercantilism → chokepoint retaliation → truce fragility):** US chip controls → China rare-earth controls (Oct 2025) → one-year truce → **second-order: the Nov 27, 2026 truce expiry becomes a scheduled flashpoint (F8) that neither side can fully de-escalate because both chokepoints are load-bearing.**

- **CHAIN D (Wide-shallow AI → concentration → inequality):** Anthropic shows adoption concentrates among high-tenure users/high-income regions → benefits accrue to capital and skilled labor → **second-order: entry-level roles most exposed → political pressure for both AI restriction and mercantilist "jobs" justifications for tariffs → reinforces Chain A.**

- **CHAIN E (Statistical vs real decoupling):** Bilateral US-China trade falls → but demand reroutes through Vietnam/India/Mexico (US-India goods deficit **+27% to $58.2B in 2025**) → **second-order: rising deficits with connectors invite the *next* tariff round (US-India already saw 50% rates), making connector status temporary (F13).**

*(Chart concept: a two-axis diagram — X = "AI capital intensity / bond issuance," Y = "effective tariff rate & export-control intensity" — with the 2026–2030 trajectory arrow pointing up-and-right into a shaded "durable-tariffs + over-financed-buildout + small-TFP" quadrant that the thesis names.)*

### THESIS STRESS-TEST

**The single strongest disconfirming evidence:** Anthropic's data show AI's task-penetration is **"measurably accelerating"** and coding is migrating from augmentation to automation. If the learning-curve effect compounds — high-tenure users unlocking genuinely automated workflows — the productivity J-curve could bend up *faster* than the 40-year electricity base rate, breaking the "small near-term gains" pillar (F4) and vindicating the Goldman ~1.5pp view.

**What would have to be true for the thesis to break:** (1) BLS productivity sustainably >2.5%/yr by 2028 attributable to AI; **and** (2) the bond-market strain resolving benignly (rates falling as AI monetization funds itself from cash flow, not debt); **and** (3) a durable political reversal dropping the effective tariff rate below 10%. All three would need to co-occur. The research makes each individually unlikely and their conjunction very unlikely — which is why the thesis is stated at ~high but not certain conviction.

### 3.5 Base / Upside / Downside Scenarios

- **BASE CASE (thesis holds, ~55%):** Effective tariffs stay >10% via 232/301; capex grows but a financing stress event hits by 2027; TFP gains stay <1pp/yr; truce fragile but holds through 2026. *(F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F8 all resolve as forecast.)*
