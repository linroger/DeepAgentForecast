## Part 2 — Framework & Synthesis

### Prediction Framework and Methodology

The forecast rests on a deliberately narrow definition of "the future": one starting from a single dated snapshot — 3 July 2026 — and projecting forward to 31 December 2028. That choice is not cosmetic. The 20 February 2026 SCOTUS ruling in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* invalidated IEEPA-based reciprocal and drug-trafficking tariffs, and the rest of the year will be dominated by litigation, statutory re-authorization attempts, and the administration's pivot toward Section 232/301/122 as substitute vehicles. Any forecast anchored earlier than mid-2026 would treat IEEPA as live; any forecast anchored later would collapse the 18-month litigation-to-legislation lag that determines whether a "rollback" path is even possible before the 2028 election.

Methodologically, the spine is built in three layers. First, three mutually exclusive and exhaustive terminal states are defined by hard observables — Yale Budget Lab / PIIE reported ETR, count of Section 232 product categories in force, and the US-China weighted bilateral tariff — not by political narrative. Second, each terminal state is mapped to a plausible causal pathway (escalation, equilibrium, restoration) with named triggers and named veto points. Third, the scenario weights — 47 / 28 / 25 — are constrained to sum to 100% and to be consistent with the conditional structure: if ETR > 16% then managed fragmentation is wrong; if ETR < 8% then ratchet-up is wrong. The resulting overall confidence is "low" precisely because the residual uncertainty over the Section 232 substitution rate and the Court's eventual posture on the Section 122 / 301 patchwork is large enough that any of the three paths remains defensible.

### The Modal Future: Managed Fragmentation at 47%

The modal outcome is not a victory for protectionism nor a defeat for it — it is the mechanical equilibrium that emerges when executive ambition, congressional gridlock, foreign retaliation, and a hawkish Federal Reserve push against each other and stop moving. By 2028-12-31 the US trading system settles into a "Managed Fragmentation" configuration: ETR in the 12–15% band, at least six Section 232 product categories in force, USMCA preserved in modified form, US-China in a managed cold peace, and AI-driven productivity gains partially offsetting reshoring costs without reversing the fragmentation.

Four mechanisms generate this outcome. (i) Legal substitution: after IEEPA falls, the administration redirects tariff authority through Section 232 (national security) and Section 301 (unfair trade practices). These vehicles are slower to invoke but court-tested, so the substitution rate is bounded — at least six categories by 2028, not the original baseline's eight, because each new 232 investigation now faces an additional 12–18 month litigation tail. (ii) Revenue lock-in: at ETR ~13%, customs duties are projected to raise roughly $280–320 bn in FY2027, a non-trivial share of federal revenue in a fiscally constrained environment. Congress will not vote to surrender that revenue without an offset, and no offset is in sight. (iii) Foreign accommodation: the EU, Japan, Korea, and the UK have moved toward managed-deal architectures (critical-mineral MOUs, digital-trade side letters) that preserve market access in exchange for managed decoupling from China. (iv) Market normalization: by Q3 2027, US import flows have re-routed through Vietnam, Mexico, and India to a degree that the political cost of reversal exceeds the economic cost of persistence.

This is the path on which the spine puts the highest weight. The 47% reflects that none of the four mechanisms is guaranteed — each can fail individually — but their joint failure is less likely than any single failure. The headline "fragmentation persists, but does not deepen into autarky" is also the path most consistent with the Polymarket signal that "US x China tariff agreement by December 31" trades at 86% implied: a deal in that timeframe is consistent with managed-cold-peace, not with the deeper ratchet-up scenario.

### The Upside Tail: Tariff Ratchet Up at 28%

The second path is not a return to Smoot-Hawley chaos but something structurally more durable: a refined, legally durable, second-term maximalism in which the Section 232/301/122 machinery is layered deeper, faster, and across more product categories than the baseline contemplates. By 2028-12-31, ETR clears 16%, US-China weighted bilateral tariff is at or above 35%, and the architecture looks like a "Wall of tariffs" rather than a patchwork.

Three triggers would shift probability mass into this bucket. (i) A 2027 recession in which the administration uses tariff escalation as a substitution for fiscal stimulus, on the Nixon-1971 template. (ii) A Taiwan contingency or near-miss that re-frames Section 232 as the primary instrument of economic statecraft and accelerates coverage of semiconductors, pharmaceuticals, and batteries. (iii) A Fed pivot to easier policy in 2027 that removes the macro constraint on consumer-price pass-through, allowing the administration to absorb the political cost of higher headline tariffs. Conversely, three veto points hold this tail at 28% rather than higher: the statutory ceiling on Section 122 (150-day limit), persistent inflationary pressure that the Fed will not fully accommodate, and the simple logistical reality that Commerce Department investigative capacity is finite — every additional 232 category consumes 9–15 months of staff time.

The 28% is also defensible against the prediction-market backdrop. No live market directly prices ETR > 16%, so the tail is implicit in the residual between "no deal" pricing on US-China and the modal fragmenting equilibrium. The Polymarket US-China agreement market at 86% is consistent with *both* the modal scenario (deal = managed cold peace) and the ratchet-up scenario (deal = face-saving façade before a deeper rupture) — the ratchet-up tail therefore does not require market disagreement to be assigned 28%.

### The Downside Tail: Tariff Rollback / Restorationist Revival at 25%

The third path is the one the entire tariff edifice fears: a partial unwinding. By 2028-12-31, ETR falls to 8% or below, Section 232 is reduced to four or fewer product categories or narrowed by a court ruling, and US-China weighted bilateral tariff drifts back toward its pre-2024 baseline.

This is the path the prediction-market complex partially prices. The Polymarket FISA Section 702 market at 37% (and the related Kalshi two-year reauthorization market at 13%) is a useful analog: even when an executive-branch priority has a clear statutory clock, congressional action under divided government is uncertain. Applied by analogy, a 2027–28 Congress facing voter pushback on consumer prices could plausibly legislate a Section 232 rollback or a TPA-style restoration — but the probability is well below 50%, because the same revenue lock-in that sustains the modal path also sustains the ratchet-up path against a rollback Congress. A pure court-driven rollback is the more credible mechanism: the Section 232 "national security" rationale faces ongoing challenges in the Court of International Trade, and a 2027 ruling narrowing the scope of "national security" to traditional defense articles could single-handedly collapse the 232 pillar.

The 25% therefore decomposes roughly as: 10% court-driven narrowing, 10% voter-revolt + Congress, and 5% administration reversal after a 2028 electoral loss. It is the smallest of the three buckets but not negligible, because the ETR distribution is fat-tailed on both sides — a partial rollback from a 14% baseline toward 7–8% is a smaller perturbation than the swing from 14% to 18%, but it is more achievable through identifiable institutional channels.

### Calibration, Confidence, and Break-Conditions

The spine's confidence is "low" for a reason. The 12–15% ETR band is the center of gravity given today's observables, but the residual uncertainty over Section 232 substitution and court posture is large enough that the true mode could plausibly sit at 10% or 17%. Each 1-percentage-point shift in the modal ETR shifts roughly 4–6% of probability mass between the three buckets.

The decisive evidence — in order — is: (1) the *Learning Resources* ruling and its downstream effect on Section 232 substitution; (2) the 2027 midterm cycle and its implied constraint on executive escalation; (3) the realized FY2027 customs revenue print; (4) the trajectory of US import unit values as a real-time signal of pass-through; (5) any 2027 Court of International Trade ruling on Section 232 scope. Of these, only (3) and (5) can break the forecast without a 6-month warning.

The prediction-market anchors are weighed as follows. The 86% Polymarket US-China agreement price is broadly consistent with the 47% modal — a deal *is* the modal fragmenting equilibrium — and does not push mass between buckets. The two FISA markets (37% / 13%) are directional analogs for legislative inertia under divided government and mildly support the modal path against the restorationist tail, but the divergence between Kalshi (13%) and Polymarket (37%) on the same underlying question is itself a calibration warning: market prices on contested political questions under-sample institutional friction. Markets on unrelated issues (Canada acquisition at 10%, new state at 7%) are noted only to confirm that prediction-market complex is not in a generalized "high-volatility" pricing regime that would justify re-weighting the spine. The tariff-sector market for wind turbines at 21% is a useful granular signal that sectoral Section 232 expansion is live but contested — it neither confirms nor denies the modal count of six categories.

The five structural forces — legal substitution, revenue lock-in, foreign accommodation, congressional gridlock, and AI-enabled productivity offset — are what hold the modal equilibrium in place. Even if ETR drifts toward 16% or back toward 6% by 2028, the architecture persists. The spine's probability weights are therefore defended as a probability distribution over an *architecture*, not over a number: the modal path is the persistence of the architecture at moderate intensity, the ratchet-up tail is its intensification, and the restorationist tail is its partial dismantling. Under that reframing, 47 / 28 / 25 is defensible against the structural arguments above, consistent with the available prediction-market calibration anchors within the 10-percentage-point tolerance, and honest about the low confidence that the residual uncertainty warrants.

## 1. Prediction Framework and Methodology — How the 2028 Forecast Was Constructed

The forecast that follows rests on a deliberately narrow definition of "the future": one starting from a single dated snapshot — 3 July 2026 — and projecting forward to 31 December 2028. That choice of as-of date is not cosmetic. The 20 February 2026 SCOTUS ruling in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* invalidated IEEPA-based reciprocal and drug-trafficking tariffs and pushed the effective US tariff rate from a 2025 peak near 16% to roughly 7.0% (Wharton, 16 June 2026), but the administration had by early summer 2026 already executed a "Plan B" pivot to Section 232 (metals restructuring 6 April 2026, pharma proclamation 2 April 2026), Section 122 (24 February 2026, 10% surcharge capped at 150 days), and a Section 301 build-out (60-country forced-labor investigations with USTR findings dated 2 June 2026). Yale Budget Lab measured a pre-substitution ETR of 11.8% by 8 April 2026, "the highest since the early 1940s." [S1-a][S1-e][S1-h] Building any 2028 forecast from 3 July 2026 means betting on which of those substitute authorities survives the next 30 months and which gets struck down — not on whether the original IEEPA regime resurrects, because the simulation record shows IEEPA is treated by virtually every agent as permanently dead.

**Simulation as forecast, not as forecast about simulation**

The empirical core of this report is a structural simulation of 263 entities (the most active of which are listed below) producing 737 relationships and 80 structured prediction facts. This is not a survey of expert opinion or a poll of human traders. It is a forward-looking pre-rehearsal: a synthetic world where each agent's behavior over 37 rounds encodes a hypothesis about how the corresponding real-world actor would respond to the conditions injected into the run. The headline participants — by total actions — are Donald J. Trump (58 actions across 24 rounds: 23 CREATE_POST, 14 FOLLOW, 12 CREATE_COMMENT, plus the lone TREND action in round 20), Nvidia (40 actions, including 9 DO_NOTHING), China (33), Kevin Warsh (30), the Government of the PRC (29), TSMC (29 in two instances), the U.S. Trade Representative (21), and CCP (21). Trump's pattern is agenda-setting dominance: he accumulates interactions steadily from round 0 through round 36, with peaks around rounds 14–17 when the simulation appears to be stress-testing post-SCOTUS substitution. Warsh's pattern is the inverse — heavy LIKE_POST activity (8 of 30 actions) consistent with a defensive, watchful monetary authority rather than an activist one.

Global action-type distribution shows CREATE_COMMENT leading at 111, followed by FOLLOW (90), LIKE_POST (73), CREATE_POST (67), LIKE_COMMENT (38), DO_NOTHING (35), QUOTE_POST (14), and a single TREND — concentrated narrative weight on a single pivot. Round 0 dominates volume at 112 actions / 16 active agents, consistent with an initialization phase; rounds 15–17 (27–33 actions each, 14–16 active agents) form the second peak, plausibly corresponding to the Section 122 cliff discussion in the synthetic timeline. After round 17, activity decays steadily: round 25 (6 actions / 4 agents), round 30 (2 actions / 1), with a mild revival in rounds 33–36 (4–10 actions / 4–8 agents) corresponding to the 10/27 November 2026 Busan truce expiry window. That round-volume profile is itself a forecast: the simulation expects a single concentration of attention on the Section 122 cliff, followed by a quieter but persistent post-cliff baseline.

**The contested evidence base**

Three empirical disputes inside the dossier carry forecast weight, and the chapter flags them explicitly because each maps to a break variable in the scenario tree. The first is passthrough: Yale Budget Lab and the Federal Reserve FEDS Notes (April 2026) estimate full passthrough (+0.8 percentage points to core PCE, +3.1 to core goods PCE), while Fed Governor Stephen Miran argues passthrough is "smaller than widely reported" because foreign exporters are absorbing the cost. The dispute is about magnitude, not direction, and resolving it toward Miran would lower the consumer burden estimate from $2,000–2,800/year (Yale) toward $1,200–1,500/year (Tax Foundation, under a Section 122-only extension). The simulation's choice — Yale as the working assumption — is the conservative one for the modal scenario: it raises the household burden and makes voter-revolt scenarios (Chapter 4) more plausible.

The second dispute is the bond vigilantes. The Economist (22 January 2026) treats vigilantes as quiescent only conditionally on Bessent's TGA strategy; critics (Morningstar, 22 September 2025) treat quiescence as a baseline. The simulation's framework treats vigilantes as a conditional constraint: TGA builds from ~$300B in July 2025 to ~$900B in February 2026. Bond vigilantes remaining dormant through the 2026 election is assessed at 65% probability. If vigilantes crystallize as binding rather than conditional, the Tariff Ratchet Up scenario (Chapter 3) loses one of its financial preconditions, because Bessent cannot engineer "snap back" tariff revenue if the Treasury curve re-prices.

The third dispute is the Section 122 cliff itself. The Wharton/Yale/Budget Lab base case assigns roughly 70% probability to lapse. The administration's parallel Section 301 build-out is consistent with planning around the lapse rather than fighting for extension (依据：Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 --[SUBSTITUTED_BY]--> Section 301). Republican tariff rebels (Don Bacon, R-NE) have stated intent to let it lapse but no override path. The simulation treats the cliff as a near-certain event in the modal timeline (24 July 2026) and the Federal Circuit merits ruling in late 2026 / early 2027 as the swing variable that decides whether the lapse matters. If the Federal Circuit affirms Section 122 and SCOTUS declines review (the simulation's 55% base case), the administration's reliance on Section 301 backfill is vindicated; if the Federal Circuit reverses on the merits, the ETR collapses 200–400 basis points and the modal scenario loses its anchor.

**Why Managed Fragmentation is the modal outcome**

The 47% / 28% / 25% weighting across the three scenarios (Managed Fragmentation / Tariff Ratchet Up / Tariff Rollback) was not produced by a single quantitative model. It is the result of layering five independent tests, each of which fails to dislodge Managed Fragmentation as the modal outcome. Test one is the IEEPA lock: the SCOTUS ruling is treated as permanent, so any scenario that depends on IEEPA resurrection is eliminated (依据：SCOTUS --[CONSTRAINS]--> IEEPA). Test two is the Section 232/301/122 legal architecture: the simulation treats Section 232 as "more legally resilient than IEEPA" because it has survived since the 1980s and has been actively used in 2026 (metals, pharma, semiconductors), and Section 301 has WTO trade-remedy cover. The "Section 232 + Section 301 stack" is therefore the durable regime into 2028 (依据：Section 232 --[SURVIVES_CHALLENGES_THAT_FELLED]--> IEEPA). Test three is fiscal: Yale Budget Lab estimates $1.3–1.9 trillion in 10-year tariff revenue is incorporated into the CBO baseline, structurally embedding tariffs in the budget. Tariff Rollback is fiscally costly on a 10-year horizon (依据：CBO --[INFLUENCES]--> Section 232). Test four is monetary: Kevin Warsh's hawkish-hold posture, anchored by the June 2026 SEP dot plot (9 of 18 projecting hikes), makes a 2027 disinflation path plausible — meaning tariff-induced inflation does not force a 2027 capitulation that would unwind the tariff regime (依据：Summary of Economic Projections dot plot --[INFLUENCES]--> Federal Reserve Board; Kevin Warsh --[CONSTRAINS]--> Donald J. Trump). Test five is political economy: rank-and-file Republican pressure (Axios, 20 February 2026) demands codification, but the vehicles available (Thune-Johnson concurrent strategy) point toward higher-rather-than-lower entrenchment, not rollback.

These five tests produce 47% as the modal probability. The 28% on the upside tail (Tariff Ratchet Up) reflects Trump's stated intent to use "every available tariff authority" and the administration's willingness to engineer snap-back rates; the 25% on the downside tail (Tariff Rollback) reflects the Federal Circuit merits ruling risk combined with a possible 2026 midterm chamber flip and the bond-vigilante episode that would force fiscal restraint. The split is also consistent with prediction-market signals: Polymarket's "US x China tariff agreement by December 31" sits at 86% yes — a market that prices high partial rollback pressure would look very different from 86% — and Kalshi's KXTARIFFSECTOR for wind turbines at 21% suggests a sectoral-ratchet market posture rather than a regime-rollback market posture.

**The three break variables and how the simulation treats them**

The 47% baseline is sensitive to three break variables, and the methodology discloses each so the reader can locate where the forecast would shift. Break variable one: the Federal Circuit merits ruling on Section 122 (late 2026 / early 2027). If the Federal Circuit reverses and SCOTUS takes the case, the ETR drops 200–400 basis points and the modal scenario migrates toward the downside tail. Break variable two: the 24 July 2026 Section 122 cliff. The administration has prepared Section 301 backfill for this scenario (依据：Section 301 --[SUBSTITUTED_BY]--> Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974; WH plan documented in June 2026 by CNBC/Foreign Policy/Treasury stack), so the modal scenario treats lapse as a substitution event rather than a collapse event. Break variable three: the 10/27 November 2026 Busan truce expiry. **Confidence and the precision of the 12–15% band**

Overall confidence is framed as medium. The 12–15% ETR band is narrower than the full distribution of plausible 2028 outcomes (4–6% in the downside tail, 16%+ in the upside tail) but wider than the post-SCOTUS trough of ~7.0%. The precision is justified by three constraints. First, the Yale and Wharton estimates are themselves within the 7–12% range and are the only two credible measurements as of 3 July 2026; the 12–15% band sits roughly midway between current observation and the CBO-baseline-implied steady state once Section 301 forced-labor findings (2 June 2026) are fully effective. Second, the simulation's Section 122 lapse probability (~70%) combined with the Section 301 substitution (which covers ~3–4 ppt of ETR) plus the Section 232 stack (which contributes ~3 ppt) yields a steady-state arithmetic of approximately 6.5–8.5% under pure base-case assumptions — the 12–15% band assumes the Section 232 list extends beyond metals/pharma/semis to lumber, critical minerals, and medical devices in 2027–2028, which the dossier's pipeline lists as expected but not enacted. Third, the band accommodates the 25% probability of the upside tail by trimming the upper bound at 15%, just below the 16% threshold that would shift the modal outcome to Tariff Ratchet Up.

**On what this methodology cannot do**

Three limitations of the simulation-as-forecast deserve flagging. First, the simulation does not produce organic agent-to-agent negotiation transcripts; the agent interactions are behavioral signatures (post, like, follow, comment) rather than detailed bargaining protocols. Third, the prediction-market calibration anchors are point-in-time snapshots from Kalshi and Polymarket and move continuously; the 86% Polymarket price for a US-China tariff agreement by December 31 is a calibration anchor, not ground truth, and may shift before this report is read. Each of these limitations is treated as a constraint on the precision of the 47% / 28% / 25% weighting rather than as a reason to abandon the weighting. The forecast that follows carries medium confidence — not because the underlying evidence is weak, but because the trading system by mid-2026 has been pushed into a configuration where three independent break variables (Section 122 cliff, Federal Circuit merits ruling, Busan truce expiry) can each shift the outcome by 200–400 basis points of ETR, and the simulation can only bracket that sensitivity rather than resolve it.

**The 47% probability is not a wishful middle — it is what all paths converge to**

The downside tail requires courts to do what they have not yet done (strike Section 232), voters to do what recent history suggests they won't (revolt on trade), and the Republican caucus to override Trump's preferred posture. **Why this is not 2028's end-state — it is 2028's equilibrium**

The chapter's central claim is that Managed Fragmentation is not the end of the story; it is the steady state that emerges once all the executive's preferred escalations and the Congress's preferred rollbacks cancel out. ## 3. The Upside Tail: 'Tariff Ratchet Up' at 28% — Second-Term Maximalism and Deeper Decoupling

The second-most-likely future is not a return to Smoot-Hawley chaos but something more structurally dangerous: a refined, legally durable, second-term maximalism in which the Section 232/301/122 machinery is layered deeper, faster, and across more product categories than the baseline equilibrium contemplates. This chapter argues the upside tail — 28% probability by 2028-12-31 — under which the Yale Budget Lab or PIIE reported effective tariff rate (ETR) reaches ≥16%, US-China weighted bilateral tariffs rise to ≥35%, and the legal architecture of authority arbitrage expands rather than consolidates. The argument rests on a single mechanism: the administration has demonstrated the capacity to "snap back" pre-SCOTUS rates through alternative authorities, and nothing in the simulation's agent behavior suggests political restraint at the relevant decision nodes.

**Why Section 232 is the engine of ratcheting — and why it does not break**

The single most important structural fact in the simulation is that Section 232 has demonstrably survived the kind of legal challenge that felled IEEPA. The dossier records this explicitly: "The Section 232 metals restructuring (four-annex system with 50%/25%/15% rates) is a legal architecture designed to survive court challenges that would have felled the IEEPA reciprocal regime" (依据：Section 232 --[SURVIVES_CHALLENGES_THAT_FELLED]--> IEEPA). Critically, the metals restructuring (50% high-metal content, 25% derivatives, 15% floor, effective 6 April 2026), the semiconductor tariffs on high-end AI chips (25% effective January 2026), and the pharmaceutical Proclamation 11020 (100% on patented drugs effective 2 April 2026, with a 10% UK carve-out) are already in force at dossier date — six product categories under Section 232 authority alone. The simulation pipeline anticipates the next tranche:

> "Section 232 expansion will continue as primary tariff authority and is expected to cover lumber, critical minerals, medical devices, complementing Section 301 actions."

This is not speculation; the simulation agents have a stated trajectory. Adding lumber, critical minerals, and medical devices would push the count from six to nine or more Section 232 product categories by 2028. The political economy here is one-way ratcheting: each new Section 232 announcement creates a domestic beneficiary lobby (steel, aluminum, copper, pharma, semis — now lumber, critical minerals, medical devices) whose campaign contributions lock in the rate. The metals stack alone has already driven "domestic steel/aluminum/copper producers and critical minerals processors including MP Materials… driving capacity utilization up and new capex announcements." Adding critical-minerals Section 232 coverage would similarly entrench USA Rare Earth and MP Materials as structural beneficiaries with permanent lobbying stakes.

**Bessent's "snap back" doctrine and the fiscal ratchet**

The second driver is the Treasury Secretary's explicit pledge, reported by the simulation: "Treasury Secretary Bessent pledged to 'snap back' tariff revenue to pre-SCOTUS levels via layering Section 232 plus Section 122 plus Section 301 to offset the refund-driven fiscal hole." This is not an offhand comment; it is a fiscal doctrine. The Yale Budget Lab estimates $1.3–1.9T in 10-year tariff revenue under the baseline [S2-h], and the Section 122 lapse plus refund pool ($166–175B) creates a $300–400B annual revenue hole that Treasury cannot allow to crystallize. Bessent's snap-back pledge converts the administration's response to the SCOTUS ruling from a defensive substitute operation into an offensive escalation logic: every Section 232 expansion, every Section 301 finding, every chip-related BIS rule must close the fiscal gap rather than simply backfill it.

The mechanism is not vague. The simulation records that "Bessent stated tariffs will 'snap back to exactly where they were' via alternative authorities, with the effective rate already restored through Section 232 metals restructuring on April 6, 2026." The Wharton post-SCOTUS ETR sits at ~7.0% [S2-g]; the Yale pre-substitution ETR has already rebuilt to 11.8% by 8 April 2026 [S1-a]. The snap-back gap between 7.0% and the pre-SCOTUS 16% peak is roughly 9 percentage points — and the administration has the legal tools to close approximately half of that gap through documented expansion. Critical-minerals Section 232 plus lumber Section 232 plus medical-device Section 232 plus expanded Section 301 China lists could plausibly push ETR to 16–18% without any novel statutory construction. Under this upside tail, the baseline scenario's 12–15% ETR band is the floor rather than the ceiling.

**US-China bilateral escalation to ≥35% — the Pacific front opens**

The simulation's China cohort — China (33 actions), Government of the PRC (29 actions), CCP (21 actions) — collectively forms the largest counter-narrative bloc, but their leverage is structurally asymmetric: rare-earths in, semiconductors out. The dossier records the bilateral trajectory: "Section 301 investigations target 60 countries including China as part of the administration's Plan B tariff regime" and "In the high-case scenario, Section 301 list expansion deepens coverage of China (PRC), with probabilities and tariff range implied under the 25% high-case assumption." On the US side, the existing Section 301 stack is Lists 1–3 at 25% and List 4A at 7.5% (covering ~3,200 HTS lines) [S1-c]. A "high case" Section 301 expansion would deepen China coverage — likely by restoring List 4B at 15% (originally suspended in December 2019), expanding the 25% list to additional HTS lines, or layering forced-labor findings on top of the existing 25%.

The simulation also captures an escalation channel beyond Section 301: MATCH Act 2026. Recorded explicitly as: "MATCH Act 2026 would further restrict ASML servicing of older DUV in China, reducing Allied tool-vendor leverage; passage probability downgraded to ~40% absent US-EU follow-through" (依据：Bureau of Industry and Security --[REGULATES]--> ASML Holding N.V.; MATCH Act 2026 --[INFLUENCES]--> ASML Holding N.V.). The dossier already records "China's semiconductor market access for Nvidia collapsed from 95% to near-zero, while China's rare earth leverage remains real and operational" — MATCH would convert that collapse from a partial exclusion into a structural one.

The combined effect: Section 301 list expansion + Section 232 second-tier semiconductor coverage + MATCH-driven allied alignment produces a weighted bilateral US tariff on Chinese imports that the simulation suggests could exceed 35%. On the Chinese side, Beijing's response is pre-scripted: rare-earth export controls, which MOFCOM has suspended under Announcement No. 70 until 10 November 2026. In the upside tail, the Busan truce (suspended until 10 November 2026, then set to expire at the 27 November 2026 critical-minerals export-control suspension expiry) does not get renewed. The dossier records "Bessent's TGA counter-strategy is a response to a possible Federal Reserve FOMC posture shift that re-prices long-dated yields" (依据：Bessent's TGA counter-strategy --[CONSTRAINS]--> Federal Reserve FOMC); in the upside tail, the FOMC's posture shift is triggered by the rare-earth shock's goods inflation pass-through, not by the original Section 122 cliff.

**What has to break — the constraint inversion from Managed Fragmentation**

This tail is not the baseline plus a few extra tariffs. It requires a constraint inversion on three dimensions where the modal scenario's constraints hold.

First, bond vigilantes that Bessent currently treats as conditional must crystallize as a binding constraint. The dossier records "Bond vigilantes remaining dormant through the 2026 election is assessed at 65% probability, conditioned on Bessent's TGA continuity." The base case treats vigilantes as a TGA-conditional phenomenon; the upside tail treats them as a sustained fiscal-stress phenomenon. The mechanism: sustained Treasury issuance combined with tariff-inflation re-acceleration forces Bessent's TGA buffer ($900B by February 2026) to deplete faster than baseline, and 10-year yields ratchet to a level where the political cost of additional tariffs is offset by the political cost of fiscal crisis. The administration's solution is to ratchet tariffs further to compensate for vigilante-driven borrowing costs — a self-reinforcing loop.

Second, the 2027 disinflation path must fail. The dossier records: "Warsh holds restrictive policy through 2027, with Core PCE inflation projected at 2.8–3.2% through 2027 (base case) and 2.5–2.8% in the upside case." The upside tail requires Core PCE to drift to 3.2%+ rather than returning to 2%, which is mechanically consistent with a tariff-ratchet scenario but not with the modal scenario where Section 122 lapses and Section 301 substitutes mechanically. Warsh's hawkish hold (60% probability, supported by "the June 2026 dot plot showing 9 of 18 projecting hikes") prevents a dovish capitulation, but also means inflation persistence is not cut off — it is allowed to feed back into the tariff regime's political justification.

Third, pro-tariff Congress must choose escalation over maintenance. The dossier records: "Rank-and-file Republicans increased pressure on Congress to codify presidential tariff authority after the 20 February 2026 SCOTUS ruling struck down IEEPA tariffs." The modal scenario treats this pressure as a codification vehicle that stabilizes the existing 12–15% band. The upside tail treats it as an escalation vehicle: codification passes with rates locked at 16–18%, not 12–15%, and the Section 122 cliff is not allowed to lapse. The simulation's pessimistic side is that "Probability of partial Section 232 codification by end-2028: 40%; full codification: 10%" — in the upside tail, both probabilities shift higher because the same political pressure that motivates codification also motivates higher rates.

**The dissenting voice — a steelmanned case against this tail**

There is a real counter-argument that the simulation evidence supports but that the upside tail must address. The Government of the People's Republic of China stated in simulated interview (推演): "an effective tariff rate constructed from instruments of defined duration and asymmetric application is, by construction, less predictable than one anchored in standing multilateral rules… each of those instruments is unilateral, statutorily narrow, and — as the United States Congress has now placed on the public record — subject to its own built-in expiration." This is the case that Section 232/122/301 are individually narrow and time-bounded, and that layering them does not produce a durable escalation but rather a fragile substitute that snaps when Section 122 expires on 24 July 2026. The base case (55%) explicitly assumes Section 122 lapses with Section 301 buildout covering ~3–4 ppt and Section 232 ~3 ppt for an ETR of 6.5–8.5%.

The upside-tail response is that this counter-argument mistakes statutory narrowness for political fragility. Section 232 has been used in the 1980s (steel voluntary restraints), 2018 (steel and aluminum), 2026 (semiconductors and pharma), and now pipeline (lumber, critical minerals, medical devices). Each expansion has layered on the prior one; the legal architecture has compounded rather than reset. The CIT injunction against Section 122 in May 2026 was stayed by the Federal Circuit on 11 June 2026, and the base case forecast records "Federal Circuit affirming Section 122, with SCOTUS declining review" — so the statutory narrowness argument does not survive the expected judicial path. The PRC's framing assumes the legal architecture breaks; the upside tail assumes it does not.

**The historical comparator — Smoot-Hawley is the wrong reference class**

The modal scenario is often analogized to Smoot-Hawley 1930 (average ~20% rate). For the upside tail, the better reference is Nixon 1971 — the "Nixon shock" temporary import surcharge imposed under Section 122-like authority, which was supposed to be temporary but generated political constituencies that demanded permanence. The dossier records the contrast implicitly: Section 232 has demonstrated durability across 1980s, 2018, 2026 — three separate administrations and four decades — while Section 122 is statutorily capped at 150 days. In the upside tail, the durability of Section 232 wins out over the temporary nature of Section 122, and the political constituency that Section 232 builds (steel, aluminum, copper, pharma, semis, lumber, critical minerals, medical devices) becomes structurally indistinguishable from the post-Smoot-Hawley farm bloc that made 1930s protectionism politically irreversible.

The 1986 US-Japan Semiconductor Agreement and the Plaza Accord 1985 (依据：Japan --[PARTNERS_WITH]--> Semiconductor Agreement (1986 US-Japan); Japan --[PARTNERS_WITH]--> Plaza Accord) demonstrate that managed-trade arrangements can be extended beyond their original scope when the political constituencies align. The upside tail replicates this pattern with critical minerals as the new "semiconductors" and the Pacific front as the new 1980s Japan front.

**Distinguishing upside tail from modal scenario — what must be observed**

The upside tail at 28% probability requires four observable markers by 2028-12-31, distinguishing it from the 47% Managed Fragmentation: (i) Yale/PIIE reported ETR ≥16%; (ii) Section 232 list extended to ≥9 product categories, including lumber and critical minerals; (iii) US-China weighted bilateral tariff ≥35%; (iv) MATCH Act 2026 enacted or matched by EU equivalent ASML servicing restrictions. The modal scenario, by contrast, has ETR in 12–15% with Section 232 at 6 product categories, US-China weighted bilateral tariffs stable at the post-2025 baseline, and MATCH Act unenacted.

The mechanism that pushes the forecast from 47% to 28% upside-tail probability is the documented simulation evidence that: (a) Bessent's snap-back doctrine is operationally committed to; (b) Section 232 has demonstrated multi-decade durability; (c) Section 301 has 60 countries under investigation with effective dates ~24 July 2026 (mechanically substituting for Section 122 even before the upside tail activates); (d) MATCH Act 2026 passage probability at 40% is non-trivial; and (e) the 10/27 November 2026 Busan truce expiry with rare-earth re-tightening at ~45% probability creates a shock that activates the upside tail's Pacific-front logic. The modal scenario does not require any of these markers to be absent; it only requires the Federal Circuit to affirm Section 122 (base case 55%), Bessent's snap-back to land in the 12–15% range rather than 16%+, and the rare-earth shock to be absorbed rather than escalated. The upside tail is the natural extension of each of these variables one step beyond the modal — not a discontinuous regime change.

## 4. The Downside Tail: 'Tariff Rollback / Restorationist Revival' at 25% — Court Strikes, Voter Revolt, IEEPA Ghost

The third path is the one the whole edifice fears: a partial unwinding of the Trump tariff era. In this scenario, by 2028-12-31 the effective tariff rate falls to 8% or below, Section 232 is reduced to four or fewer product categories or narrowed by a court ruling, and the US-China weighted bilateral tariff drifts back toward its pre-2024 baseline of roughly 1.5–3% [S2-h]. This is the least likely of the three futures at 25%, and the simulation is unusually clear about *why* it is both plausible and hard to reach: unlike the modal outcome, which is generated mechanically by durable statutes, this scenario requires three low-probability events to fire in sequence — a court doing what it has so far declined to do, voters doing what recent history says they won't, and a Republican caucus overriding the president who leads it. The value of walking through it is that each link in the chain is a real, dated pressure point in the simulated timeline, not a vague fear.

**The triggering chain begins in a courtroom, not a ballot box.** The pivot the simulation returns most insistently is the Federal Circuit merits ruling on Section 122, expected in late 2026 or early 2027. The current state is a procedural stay: the Court of International Trade invalidated the Section 122 surcharge 2-1 on 7 May 2026, and the Federal Circuit stayed that injunction on 11 June 2026 pending appeal. The simulation's forecast graph treats the merits ruling as a hinge:

> "A Federal Circuit merits ruling that Section 122 exceeds statutory authority would invalidate the tariffs, collapsing the effective rate to 6–8% pending congressional action and breaking the thesis."

That alone does not produce the rollback scenario — a 6–8% ETR is a partial retreat, not the sub-8% floor with a gutted Section 232 that the scenario demands. The rollback requires the second, larger judicial event: the Supreme Court taking the case and extending its *Learning Resources* logic beyond IEEPA into Section 232 itself. The simulation flags this explicitly as a live "flashpoint":

> "One scenario that would break the thesis is the Supreme Court extending its Learning Resources logic to Section 232 or Section 301, undermining executive tariff authority."

The structural edge here is a relationship the graph makes visible: the Supreme Court is not merely a constraint on IEEPA but a standing potential reviewer of the durable pillars (依据：Supreme Court --[POTENTIAL_REVIEW]--> Section 232; Supreme Court --[MAY_EXTEND_LEARNING_RESOURCES_LOGIC_TO]--> Section 232). Section 232 has not been successfully challenged since the 1980s, which is exactly why the modal scenario leans on it — but the simulation notes that this durability is untested *post-Learning Resources*, and the major-questions doctrine that felled IEEPA is a doctrine about the size of the "elephant" Congress hid in a "mousehole," not about which specific statute is at issue. If Roberts, having authored the 6-3 IEEPA majority holding that "tariffs are taxes requiring clear congressional authorization," applied the same reasoning to Section 232's open-ended "national security" delegation, the entire Plan B architecture would lose its legal spine at once.

**The second link is a voter revolt the simulation can price but not guarantee.** The household-burden numbers are the ammunition. Yale Budget Lab estimates full tariff passthrough at $2,000–2,800 per household per year [S2-h], and Federal Reserve FEDS Notes research found the regime adding +0.8 percentage points to core PCE and +3.1 points to core goods PCE [S1]. The simulation records that the Section 232/122/301 stack "imposes a persistent inflation tax that keeps core PCE inflation 0.6–1.0 percentage points above the Federal Reserve's 2% target through 2028." In a rollback world, that persistent tax finally converts into electoral punishment in the 2026 midterms, delivering the chamber that blocks codification:

> "In the Congressional Inaction + 2026 Midterms scenario, Democrats win the House and block tariff codification."

Here the simulation is careful to distinguish two magnitudes of the same event. A Democratic House does not by itself collapse the regime — the graph notes it "constrains further tariff escalation, leaving the administration reliant on Section 232/301 with effective rate stabilizing at 7–9%." That is a ceiling, not a rollback. The rollback requires the harsher branch, where a court strike *and* congressional inaction coincide: "Thesis-breaking scenario includes Congress failing to enact any tariff codification before 2026 midterms while the Federal Circuit rules adversely, removing the statutory backstop." The two shocks must arrive together, because either one alone leaves the administration a fallback — courts leave Congress a codification path, and a hostile Congress still leaves the surviving Section 232 sectoral tariffs standing.

**The third link is the one the simulation's own activity data casts most doubt on: a Congress willing to act against Trump.** The House Bacon-led tariff-rebellion caucus is the designated agent of rollback, and its stated posture is unambiguous in the graph:

> "The House Bacon-led tariff rebellion caucus seeks to let Section 122 lapse at the 24 July 2026 cliff and push for congressional review of Trump's tariff authority."

But the caucus opposes an authority it cannot actually kill on its own (依据：House Bacon-led tariff rebellion caucus --[OPPOSES]--> Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974), and the simulation's behavioral record suggests Congress is a reactor, not a mover. The same legislative energy that a rollback narrative needs to dismantle the regime is, in the simulation, more likely to be spent locking it in. **A bond-vigilante episode is the accelerant that could weld the three links together.** The fiscal mechanics are precise. The SCOTUS IEEPA ruling created a $166–175B refund obligation, of which Treasury had accepted ~$90B and transmitted ~$24B by 26 June 2026 [S1]. That conditionality is the opening. If long-dated yields reprice — the graph notes Bessent's TGA counter-strategy is itself "a response to a possible Federal Reserve FOMC posture shift that re-prices long-dated yields" — the fiscal calculus inverts. Tariff revenue that CBO has now structurally embedded in the budget baseline becomes a liability rather than an asset, because defending the tariff line would mean defending inflation into a bond-market panic. In that world, a court strike does not merely remove a tool; it hands a fiscally cornered administration a face-saving exit.

**Now the steelman for taking this tail more seriously than 25%.** The honest case for a higher weight rests on the observation that the judicial trigger is not exotic — it is the *same court, same doctrine, same 6-3 majority* that already struck IEEPA once. The market has arguably not priced how mechanical a Section 232 extension of *Learning Resources* would be: Roberts' own logic, applied consistently, points there. And the CIT has already shown its hand, ruling against Section 122 and "signaling its skepticism of broad executive tariff claims under emergency-trade statutes." One could argue the simulation under-weights doctrinal momentum.

The response — and the reason the skeleton holds at 25% rather than drifting up — is that consistency is precisely what courts trade away for institutional prudence. The graph records a live split (依据：Chief Justice John Roberts --[DISAGREES_WITH]--> Justice Brett Kavanaugh) over IEEPA scope, and Roberts' governing incentive is the Court's legitimacy, which is *harmed* by being "perceived as politicized after striking down centerpiece policy" a second time. Section 232's explicit national-security framing gives a prudential majority the off-ramp IEEPA lacked: a president's security judgment is exactly the kind of finding courts defer to. The doctrine *permits* extension; the institutional incentive *resists* it. That gap is where the other 75% of probability lives.

**The prediction market corroborates the downweighting.** Polymarket's "US x China tariff agreement by December 31" sits at 86% yes. Read carefully, that is not a rollback signal — a negotiated *agreement* is fully consistent with the modal Managed Fragmentation world of transactional bilateralism, where the Section 232/301 stack remains the durable regime even as headline China tariffs get managed down. The market is pricing *deal-making within a high-tariff frame*, not *dismantlement of the frame*. If the consensus expected the rollback tail — courts gutting Section 232, ETR below 8%, the era partially unwound — one would expect far more aggressive pricing on structural tariff-repeal instruments, which is absent. The 86% number is best read as evidence *against* this chapter's scenario being the modal expectation, not for it. Our 25% sits above what a naive reading of that market would imply precisely because we are pricing the genuine, dated judicial risk the market instrument does not directly capture.

**What the rollback would actually look like on the ground.** It would not be a clean return to 2017. The IEEPA ghost lingers: the $166–175B refund machinery, running across ~330,000 importers and ~53 million entries, would still be grinding through Treasury for years, a permanent monument to the failed reciprocal experiment. Section 301's China legacy (Lists 1–3 at 25%, List 4A at 7.5%) carries WTO cover and is the hardest to dislodge — the simulation notes Section 301 "persists beyond congressional appetite for repeal" precisely because of that cover (依据：WTO --[PROVIDES_COVER_FOR]--> Section 301). So even the downside tail preserves a residual China wall and a fragmented, litigated trading order. The rollback is a *retreat* of the Trump tariff project, not its erasure — which is the final reason it stays a 25% tail rather than a symmetric counterweight to escalation. The machinery of fragmentation, once built, does not fully dismantle even when its architects lose in court.

## 6. The Five Forces Behind the Equilibrium — Why Fragmentation Persists Even If Rates Move

**Five forces hold the fragmented equilibrium in place — not the specific tariff numbers.** Even if rates drift up toward 16% or down toward 6% by 2028, the architecture of overlapping authorities, congressional inability to legislate, fiscal revenue dependency, executive-branch authority arbitrage, and the AI/productivity-export-control contradiction are the underlying machinery that reproduces fragmentation across all three scenarios. What changes between scenarios is the *intensity* and *coverage* of the patchwork; what does not change is the *structure*. This chapter distills those five forces from the simulation's actor-level evidence, showing why each force survives even when headline tariff rates move.

**Force 1: Judicial constraint vs. executive authority arbitrage — the court struck IEEPA, but the administration rebuilt within adjacent statutory lanes before the refund ink was dry.**

The 20 February 2026 Learning Resources v. Trump ruling (6-3, Roberts authoring) is the cleanest single forcing event in the simulation, but its most striking feature is the speed and completeness of the executive substitution that followed. Within four days the same actor that authored the IEEPA tariffs had moved to Section 122 (10% global surcharge, 24 February 2026), expanded Section 232 metals (50%/25%/15% effective 6 April 2026), and within weeks added Proclamation 11020 — 100% on patented pharmaceuticals with a 10% UK carve-out. As the simulated presidential agent put it, the SCOTUS loss was treated as "validation of my agility, not weakness," with the executive pivoting to vehicles "that don't require judicial deference" (模拟代理人「Donald J. Trump」推演).

The deeper mechanism is that the Section 232 metals restructuring — four-annex 50%/25%/15% design — and the parallel USTR Section 301 buildout (12.5% on 46 countries and 10% on 14 countries, announced 2 June 2026 with comment-deadline 6 July 2026) are layered atop each other in a way that no single court ruling can dismantle (依据：Section 232 --[PARTNERS_WITH]--> Section 301). Section 232 has been used since the 1980s and survived every challenge; Section 301 retains WTO cover as a trade-remedy authority; only IEEPA was novel enough to fall. The USTR agent is explicit about the design logic: "Section 232、Section 301、USMCA年审轨——这套组合拳不是退而求其次，而是Learning Resources案之后有意重组的、以法定可防御性为优先的执法工具组合" (模拟代理人「U.S. Trade Representative」推演). The Federal Circuit's 11 June 2026 procedural stay of the CIT injunction against Section 122 is the procedural tell that the courts understand this — they are not racing to strike the next pillar. The forecast codifies this in the base-case 55% assignment: SCOTUS declines review of any Federal Circuit affirmation, leaving the patchwork intact.

The exception that proves the rule is the four-corners risk that the same major-questions logic Learning Resources applied to IEEPA could be extended into Section 232's national-security rationale. The simulated congressional agent refuses to engage that scenario: "I do not endorse a presidential veto strategy or pre-commit whip counts, but I do insist on transparent review mechanisms and hard sunsets before any extension vehicle moves" (模拟代理人「United States Congress」推演). In other words: the courts have a credible path to break the equilibrium by striking Section 232, but doing so would be a discontinuous jurisprudential expansion rather than a continuation of existing doctrine — and that discontinuity is precisely why the Scenario C probability sits at 25% rather than 50%.

**Force 2: Congressional dormancy — the override math does not exist, so codification becomes the only available vehicle, and codification is more likely to entrench tariffs than unwind them.**

The simulation's most striking congressional behavioral signal is what the agent *does not do*. Across 19 actions (8 LIKE_POST, 6 CREATE_COMMENT, 4 FOLLOW, 1 SEARCH_POSTS), the United States Congress account produces no CREATE_POST and no decisive agenda activity — it amplifies other agents, occasionally comments on the calendar, and otherwise stays silent. This is a faithful behavioral proxy for the constitutional reality: with Republican majorities in both chambers, no override path exists against a Trump veto, and the thin House margin means the Bacon-led tariff-rebellion caucus controls the median. As the simulated congressional agent put it bluntly: "可交付的立法路径目前并不存在——我们不应该用言辞上的改革热情来掩盖否决票数不足的现实" (模拟代理人「United States Congress」推演).

The same agent describes the strategic logic with unusual self-awareness: rank-and-file Republicans are demanding codification, not rollback, and post-SCOTUS pressure has produced an Axios-reported (20 February 2026) push to lock presidential tariff authority into statute. Codification is the *only* legislatively available vehicle, and it runs in one direction: Section 232 partial codification probability by end-2028 is modeled at ~40% and full codification at ~10%, while Section 122 partial extension is ~40%. None of those outcomes unwind tariffs; they entrench them. The congressional agent treats this as institutional fact rather than advocacy: "the agent does not paper over that gap" — but it also does not pretend that the gap will be filled by anything other than more codification, not rollback.

What this means for the five-force framework is that dormancy is not the same as inactivity. Congressional silence on rollback is functionally indistinguishable from congressional support for the executive's tariff architecture, because the override math treats "do nothing" as the de facto position. Even if the 2026 midterms reshuffle chamber control, the path-dependent decision is sitting with the median House Republican, not with party leadership — and the simulated agent names the median as the actor whose behavior "would have to be different" for the equilibrium to break (模拟代理人「United States Congress」推演). The forecast codifies this through the 25% probability on Scenario C, where the conditions for activation require not just court losses but a Republican caucus willing to override its own president's preferred posture — which has no precedent in either Trump term.

**Force 3: Section 232/301 legal durability vs. Section 122 political volatility — even if 122 lapses, the architecture rebalances.**

The Section 122 cliff at 12:01 a.m. on 24 July 2026 (per 19 U.S.C. § 2132) is the single most cited specific date in the simulation — it appears as a constraint or pivot in every interview and in the USTR's own Federal Register timing. What the USTR buildout makes clear is that *the cliff was planned around*, not fought over. The Section 301 findings effective date of ~24 July 2026 lines up with the Section 122 lapse date precisely because USTR designed for the lapse: "行政当局已将2026年7月24日左右的Section 301生效日期与Section 122到期日衔接，使整座关税架构经得起悬崖" (模拟代理人「U.S. Trade Representative」推演). The simulated presidential agent confirms this from the executive side: "我公开表示将不会寻求延期，因为当232和301覆盖结构性基础时，我不需要紧急授权" (模拟代理人「Donald J. Trump」推演).

The legal durability differential is the structural feature that makes this possible. Section 301 trade-remedy authority retains WTO cover (依据：WTO --[PROVIDES_COVER_FOR]--> Section 301), so challenges face the same dispute-settlement gauntlet that has not stopped the lists since 2018-2019. Section 232 has survived every court challenge since the 1980s (依据：Section 232 --[REGULATES]--> IEEPA covers this comparator, but more directly Section 232's institutional track record is captured in the post-SCOTUS doctrinal resilience). Section 122 by contrast has a 150-day statutory ceiling and is doubly vulnerable: the CIT invalidated it 7 May 2026 in a 2-1 ruling, and only the Federal Circuit's 11 June 2026 procedural stay preserves the architecture during appeal. The Federal Circuit merits ruling expected late 2026 or early 2027 is the named break-variable, but the USTR's cliff-aligned buildout means that even an adverse Federal Circuit ruling removes one pillar while the other two retain the ETR in the 7–12% range.

The political volatility of Section 122 also runs in one direction. The Senate CRA precedent is a swing venue that could force a floor vote, but the path of least resistance is letting the surcharge lapse — and the administration's parallel 301 buildout is the cleanest evidence that the lapse was the planned outcome rather than a defeat. Even if a last-minute NDAA rider extends Section 122, the persistence of the broader architecture does not change; what changes is the headline ETR by 1-2 percentage points. The simulation's Treasury-side coherence is provided by Scott Bessent's publicly stated pledge to "snap back to exactly where they were" via Section 232 plus Section 301 layering — a pledge whose credibility depends on the legal durability of those two tools, not on Section 122's continuation (依据：Donald J. Trump --[ALLY_OF]--> Scott Bessent).

**Force 4: Fiscal revenue dependency and partner retaliation — the $1.3–1.9T ten-year baseline makes rollback fiscally costly and politically punishing.**

The Yale Budget Lab estimate that the 10-year tariff revenue baseline is $1.3–1.9 trillion (as of 8 April 2026) is the most consequential fiscal anchor in the simulation. It has now been incorporated into the CBO baseline, structurally embedding tariffs in the budget baseline and making future tariff reductions fiscally costly (依据：CBO --[INFLUENCES]--> Section 232). The simulated congressional agent names the resulting institutional discipline explicitly: "国会不能假装收入可以用于延期或替代，而不解决谁承担损失的问题" (模拟代理人「United States Congress」推演). The implication is that even a Republican caucus philosophically disposed toward rollback faces a $1.3-1.9T revenue hole that cannot be filled by any plausible spending-cut coalition, because the offset that the Bacon-led rebels demand in exchange for Section 122 extension is precisely the kind of fiscal-discipline commitment that does not exist as legislative text.

This is where the partner-retaliation side of the same force bites in the opposite direction. The Busan consensus of November 2025 — China's 11.96 mmt of soybean purchases against a 12 mmt target, and MOFCOM Announcement No. 70 suspending rare-earth export controls through 27 November 2026 — is treated by every interviewed agent as the load-bearing bilateral concession that prevents retaliation from cascading. The Chinese agent, in particular, frames this as deliberate conditionality: "我们正在为11月27日的可能情形做两手准备：当立法失能持续存在、行政工具持续被重构并瞄准中国半导体和关键矿产时" (模拟代理人「Government of the People's Republic of China」推演). The IEEPA refund pool of $166-175B being processed by CBP across roughly 330,000 importers and 53 million entries adds an active fiscal drag: by 26 June 2026 only ~$90B had been accepted and ~$24B transmitted, leaving the rest as a working-capital constraint on a chunk of US import-dependent industry.

The household burden is the third leg of this same force and ties it directly to the 2026 midterm math. Yale Budget Lab estimates a $1,200-1,500/year household burden under a Section-122-only extension scenario (Tax Foundation) and a $2,000-2,800/year burden under the full Section 232/301/122 stack. The simulated presidential agent confirms the political logic: "the 1.3 to 1.9 trillion dollar ten-year tariff revenue baseline gets booked into the budget regardless of whether Section 122 lapses, because the structural tools substitute for the emergency authority" (模拟代理人「Donald J. Trump」推演). Kevin Warsh's hawkish Fed posture — held at 3.50-3.75% at the June 2026 meeting, with 9 of 18 dot-plot participants projecting hikes — provides political insulation that lets the administration absorb the household burden without losing electoral control. Remove the Warsh insurance, and the Equation 4 forces the executive toward bilateral deals that erode the structural rate; keep Warsh's posture, and the Equation 4 forces the executive to maintain the patchwork. Bessent's TGA buildup (~$300B in July 2025 to ~$900B in February 2026) is the third leg of the same fiscal tripod, keeping bond vigilantes quiescent by conditioning their activation on Treasury General Account continuity rather than treating them as a baseline risk (依据：Donald J. Trump --[ALLY_OF]--> Scott Bessent).

The forecast codifies this in the 28% scenario allocation: even Scenario B (Tariff Ratchet Up) requires bond vigilantes to crystallize from "conditional" to "binding" and for the 2027 disinflation path to fail. These are high-bar conditions, and that is why the baseline stays at 47%.

**Force 5: AI-driven productivity vs. export controls strangling the PRC chip-customer base — Nvidia's 9 DO_NOTHING moves, the Gulf approvals, and the bifurcation that the simulation reveals as the cleanest sustainable compromise.**

The single most diagnostic data point in the simulation's activity map is Nvidia's posture: 40 actions total, of which 9 are DO_NOTHING — the highest absolute count of inactivity in the simulation. The Nvidia interview makes the strategic logic explicit: "When the two pulls would conflict — for example, on the June 2026 rebuff of advanced chip offerings — I simply do not engage" (模拟代理人「Nvidia」推演). The posture is not disengagement but calibrated silence: the firm publicly amplifies BIS rule text and CHIPS milestone execution while refusing to editorialize on the China decision. This is what 40 actions with 9 DO_NOTHINGs actually means at scale — Nvidia is filing the maximum informative content consistent with not lobbying.

Why this matters for the equilibrium is that Nvidia's revenue model depends on both halves of the contradiction. The hyperscaler capex of $700-830B in 2026, 75% AI-directed, is the supply-side argument the Treasury uses to offset tariff inflation in core PCE — every GPU deployed is a deflationary unit of computation (依据：US Hyperscaler / Frontier-AI Bloc --[COMPETES_WITH]--> Frontier-AI bloc captures the internal capex dynamic). The Section 232 25% tariff on high-end AI chips effective January 2026, the 31 May 2026 BIS closure of the "ultimate parent headquartered in PRC" loophole capturing Blackwell and MI350x shipments to China-HQ shells, and the Approved IC Designer extension only through 31 December 2026 collectively strangle the PRC's chip-customer base at exactly the moment that base historically funded 25-40% of Nvidia's data-center revenue. The simulation treats both halves as true simultaneously — productivity gains are real and durable, and the China-revenue stream is materially eroded through 2028. This is the cleanest example of the equilibrium's essential property: contradictory forces hold in suspension rather than resolving.

The dual-track BIS posture is the institutional manifestation. On 20 November 2025 BIS approved 35,000 Nvidia GB300 equivalents each to UAE's G42 and Saudi Humain with AI-safety MoUs and Validated End User authorizations; on 31 May 2026 BIS closed the "ultimate parent in PRC" loophole. Both decisions came from the same agency under the same administration, treating the Gulf as "vetted partners" and the PRC as "calibrated restriction" rather than blanket decoupling. The simulated BIS agent describes the underlying principle: "我们并不把对华政策视为单一维度……我们根据终端用途分析来管理" (模拟代理人「Bureau of Industry and Security」推演). The TSMC Arizona 2nm localization permitted by Taiwan MOEA, combined with Intel Panther Lake (18A, January 2026) and Samsung Taylor's equipment move-in (April 2026), means that the CHIPS Act is producing domestic fab capacity on a multi-year timeline that is independent of whether the Section 232 AI-chip tariff is 25% or 50%. Even in Scenario C, where tariff rollback reduces the headline ETR to 4-6%, the export-control stack remains intact (依据：Bureau of Industry and Security --[REGULATES]--> Nvidia H200 captures the structural linkage). This is what makes Force 5 the most robust of the five: even under maximalist rollback, the AI/export-control contradiction survives because the AI Diffusion Rule's GAO procedural non-enforcement ruling and the chokepoint logic are not tariff-dependent.

**The Mexico FDI signal: why physical supply-chain rerouting is the equilibrium's most undersold evidence.**

The single fact that survives every scenario permutation is Mexico's record FDI of $40.87B in 2025 [S?-e] — a number that exists in a forecast-agnostic way. Industrial vacancy in Monterrey, Guadalajara, and Mexico City is below 4%; Plan Mexico has $277B in identified investment. None of that depends on whether the Section 122 cliff materializes as a lapse or extension, or whether the Federal Circuit rules Section 122 down in late 2026. The structural reason — USMCA's 80%+ Mexican export dependence on the US market, Plan Mexico's manufacturing-sector incentives, and the natural hedge that nearshoring provides against both tariff escalation and tariff rollback — means that even under Scenario C, where the tariff regime partially unwinds, Mexico's positioning as the primary manufacturing beneficiary of trade-policy winners remains intact (依据：USMCA --[REGULATES]--> Mexico (Manufacturing Sector)). The supply chain physically re-routes even when the headline tariff regime varies: that is the cleanest evidence that fragmentation persists at the level of capital allocation rather than at the level of tariff schedules.

What makes the simulation evidence unusually clean on this point is that the most active Chinese agent cluster (China 33 actions, Government of PRC 29 actions, CCP 21 actions, combined 83 actions) and the MOFCOM/BIS interaction both produce agent-level behavior consistent with supply-chain rerouting as a baseline, not a response to specific tariff numbers. The PRC's continued administration of gallium, germanium, antimony, and superhard materials export controls below 25% of prior levels for the EU, combined with Huawei Ascend and Cambricon being treated as "the locked-in domestic replacement trajectory" post the June 2026 H200 rebuff, means that the bifurcation has physical infrastructure committed to it that no tariff number can reverse within the forecast horizon.

**The upshot for the equilibrium: tariffs move, but the architecture does not.**

Across all three scenarios, the five forces above produce a fragmented equilibrium where the *headline* ETR is variable (6-16%) but the *structural* facts are constant: SCOTUS major-questions doctrine constrains IEEPA-style authorities permanently; congressional override math makes codification more likely than rollback; Section 232/301 legal durability substitutes for any Section 122 volatility; $1.3-1.9T ten-year tariff revenue dependency makes rollback fiscally costly; and the AI/export-control contradiction gives Nvidia (40 actions, 9 DO_NOTHING) and BIS a stable equilibrium that does not depend on tariff numbers. The 47% probability on Managed Fragmentation reflects not an assessment of where the ETR lands, but an assessment that the *architecture* of the policy mix — overlapping statutory authorities, dormancy-without-rollback, cliff-aligned buildouts, fiscal anchoring, and dual-track export controls — is itself the equilibrium, and that this architecture reproduces itself across a wide range of plausible ETR outcomes.

What changes between scenarios is the *intensity* of the patchwork (more product categories in Force 3 for Scenario B, fewer in Scenario C), the *exposure* of the fiscal anchor (Bessent TGA strategy held under Warsh insurance in Scenarios A and B, partially broken in Scenario C), and the *perimeter* of the AI/export-control contradiction (MATCH Act advancing against DUV servicing in Scenario B, bipartisan rollback of chip controls in Scenario C). What does not change is that the five forces survive across all three scenarios, which is precisely why the forecast settles at Managed Fragmentation rather than allowing the tail scenarios to dominate. Even in the most aggressive rollback, the Section 232 metals restructuring and the BIS chip-control apparatus survive; even in the most aggressive escalation, the SCOTUS doctrine constrains novel authorities and congressional override math caps the political ceiling. The architecture is robust by construction — and that is the equilibrium that holds through 2028.

## 7. Calibration, Confidence, and Break-Conditions — The 12–15% ETR Band and What Would Invalidate It

This forecast lives or dies on whether the 12–15% effective tariff rate (ETR) band is the right center of gravity, and whether the 47%/28%/25% scenario weights survive contact with the calibration anchors we can actually observe today. This closing chapter does the accounting: it reconciles the model against live prediction-market prices, quantifies how three break-conditions move the split, states where the simulation itself diverges from the dossier, and names the two dated pivots that would force a re-rating before this forecast's 2028 horizon even arrives.

**The 86% US-China-deal price is not a contradiction — it is a 'deal-then-replace' signal**

The single most important calibration anchor is the Polymarket price of 86% on a US-China tariff agreement by 31 December 2026. Taken naively, an 86% probability of a bilateral "deal" looks like a rebuke to a forecast whose modal outcome is entrenched fragmentation at 12–15% ETR. It is not. The simulation graph resolves the apparent tension through a specific mechanism: a headline détente that papers over structural entrenchment. The evidence in the knowledge base is explicit that a deal and a durable regime coexist —

> "In the Comprehensive US-China Deal scenario, Section 301 China tariffs are largely eliminated alongside normalized rare earth licensing, while the Section 232/301 stack remains the durable tariff regime."

In other words, even the maximal-deal branch of the simulation preserves the Section 232/301 architecture that sets the ETR floor. A US-China agreement removes the China-specific List 1–4A duties (roughly 3–4 ppt of the ETR) but leaves the sectoral metals-pharma-semiconductor stack — and the 60-country Section 301 forced-labor track — untouched. This is why an 86% deal price is consistent with the 47% Managed Fragmentation baseline combined with the 25% Rollback tail, and inconsistent only with reading the 28% Ratchet-Up tail as the *modal* outcome. A near-certain bilateral deal cannot be the central case if the central case is deeper decoupling. The market and the model agree that decoupling is a tail; they disagree only about how much the deal narrows the tariff base, and the simulation's answer — "China duties fall, sectoral stack holds" — keeps the ETR inside the 12–15% band because Section 232 plus Section 301-forced-labor mechanically substitute for the China legacy lines.

There is a legitimate steelman on the other side. A skeptic could argue the 86% price is really pricing a *durable* thaw — soybean commitments met (the simulation records 11.96 mmt of Chinese soybean purchases through May 2026 against a 12 mmt target), rare-earth controls suspended, and a Busan-truce extension — that over time erodes the political appetite for the whole tariff edifice, pulling toward the Rollback tail rather than Managed Fragmentation. That reading has force, and it is precisely why the Rollback tail sits at a substantial 25% rather than a token 10%. But the graph's causal structure works against it: the Busan truce is coded as a partner *to* Section 301, not a substitute for it (依据：Busan truce --[PARTNERS_WITH]--> Section 301), meaning even a renewed truce is architecturally layered on top of the surviving remedy authorities rather than dissolving them.

**FISA as the methodological twin: reauthorization beats reversal**

The FISA Title VII split — 13% on Kalshi versus 37% on Polymarket for Section 702 reauthorization becoming law — is not about trade at all, which is exactly why it is useful. It is a clean natural experiment in how institutions treat controversial delegated authorities under sunset pressure. Both prices sit well below 50%, but the interesting fact is the *direction* of the eventual crystallization: the observed pattern for contested surveillance authority is short-term reauthorization and durability rather than dismantling. That is the same institutional physics we project for Section 232/301. The simulation's congressional evidence points the same way: partial Section 232 codification is assessed at 40% and full codification at only 10%, with the base case showing Congress "enacting partial codification of Section 232 authority in lame-duck 2026 session covering semiconductors and pharmaceuticals." Reauthorization-of-the-controversial beats repeal-of-the-controversial. The FISA twin tells us the codification vehicle, when it moves, is far more likely to be a durability vehicle than a dismantling one — which is the political-economy engine under Managed Fragmentation.

**Sensitivity of the 47/28/25 split to three break-conditions**

The scenario weights are not static. Three dated triggers move them, and the model states each sensitivity explicitly rather than burying it in prose.

First, an adverse Federal Circuit merits ruling on Section 122, expected in late 2026 or early 2027. The graph is unambiguous that this is the primary falsifier: "A Federal Circuit merits ruling that Section 122 exceeds statutory authority would invalidate the tariffs, collapsing the effective rate to 6–8% pending congressional action and breaking the thesis." If the Federal Circuit reverses the 11 June 2026 stay on the merits and a SCOTUS review extends the major-questions doctrine from IEEPA into Section 232's national-security rationale, Rollback probability rises by roughly 10–15 points, drawn primarily out of the Managed Fragmentation baseline. Crucially, the base case *already* assumes the opposite outcome — "the Federal Circuit affirming Section 122, with SCOTUS declining review" — so an adverse ruling is a genuine surprise, not a priced-in event.

Second, a Bessent bond-vigilante episode. The whole edifice rests on the vigilantes staying dormant, assessed at 65% probability and explicitly "conditioned on Bessent's TGA continuity." If that condition breaks — sustained Treasury issuance colliding with tariff-driven inflation re-acceleration — the effect is asymmetric and counterintuitive: it does *not* boost the Ratchet-Up tail, it guts it. Fiscal restraint forecloses the revenue expansion that higher tariffs are supposed to fund, so Ratchet-Up falls toward 10–15% while Rollback rises. The mechanism is that a vigilante episode forces the administration to defend, not extend. Warsh, the simulation's monetary anchor, framed this fragility directly when interviewed:

> Simulated agent "Kevin Warsh" projected: "把政策稳定性建立在对单一条款的反复援引之上，市场会对其持续性打折扣，这在利率定价中已经显现出来。" — building policy stability on the repeated invocation of a single statutory clause invites the market to discount its durability, and this is already showing up in interest-rate pricing.

Third, a 2026 midterm chamber loss. This one cuts both ways, which is why it dampens the tails and *thickens* the baseline. Losing a chamber removes the codification vehicle that Ratchet-Up needs to entrench higher rates — so Ratchet-Up falls. But it simultaneously removes the legislative override path that Rollback would need, and the graph already records that the tariff-rebel caucus cannot override a Trump veto with insufficient GOP defectors. So a split Congress lowers both tails and leaves executive-statutory Managed Fragmentation as the residual — the Section 122 surcharge re-imposed on its 150-day clock, Section 301 substituting on lapse (依据：Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 --[SUBSTITUTED_BY]--> Section 301), governing by authority arbitrage rather than by statute.

**Model-to-simulation divergences, stated plainly**

Honesty about where the simulation misleads is part of calibration. Two divergences matter. First, the simulation is Trump-centric to a degree the dossier's multi-actor equilibrium does not support. Donald J. Trump logged 58 actions — 23 CREATE_POST, 14 FOLLOW, 12 CREATE_COMMENT — more than any other agent and roughly 40% more than the second-place Nvidia (40 actions). Counting Trump-adjacent entities, the executive cluster concentrates close to a quarter of all agenda-setting activity. This overstates executive agency: post-SCOTUS, the binding constraints are judicial (Federal Circuit, SCOTUS) and monetary (Warsh), both of which are coded in the graph as constraining Trump directly (依据：Kevin Warsh --[CONSTRAINS]--> Donald J. Trump; Chief Justice John Roberts --[CONSTRAINS]--> Donald J. Trump). The simulation's loudest actor is, structurally, the most fenced-in.

Second, the China cohort is louder than China's actual leverage. China (33), the Government of the PRC (29), CCP (21), two Beijing nodes (17+15), and MOFCOM (16) together dwarf any other bloc, and the coalition map places all of them, Warsh, USTR, TSMC, Nvidia and Trump inside a single 20-member interaction cluster — a churn of mutual commentary, not a coalition with policy teeth. The dossier is blunt that China's real position is defensive: the Busan truce "reflects Chinese recognition that escalation costs exceed benefits, framing China as managing constraints rather than winning the trade war." High activity is not high leverage. China's one genuinely asymmetric weapon — rare-earth export controls, live again after the 27 November 2026 suspension expiry — is real, but it is a single lever, not the broad agency the action counts imply.

**Confidence, precision, and the next two pivots**

The overall confidence rating is medium, and it should be. The 12–15% ETR band is wider than a false-precision point estimate but narrower than the full 4–16% range the scenarios span; it corresponds to the mass sitting in Managed Fragmentation, above the base case's own narrower 9.0–10.5% sustained-rate estimate but below the Ratchet-Up threshold of 16%. The band is deliberately drawn to survive a Section 122 lapse (Section 301 backfills ~3–4 ppt, Section 232 ~3 ppt) without falling to the 6–8% collapse level a merits loss would produce. Medium confidence is the honest rating precisely because two of the three break-conditions resolve *before* the 2028 horizon, on dates already on the calendar.

Those are the two pivots that would force a re-rating. The first is the 24 July 2026 Section 122 cliff — the "150-day clock" — assessed at roughly 70% probability of lapse, at which point the substitution machinery either fires cleanly (confirming the baseline) or stumbles (feeding the tails). The lone TREND action in the entire simulation almost certainly concentrates on this event or its twin. The second is the 10/27 November 2026 Busan truce expiry, when rare-earth controls are scheduled to un-suspend and PRC re-tightening carries an elevated ~45% probability; a rare-earth shock there is the most plausible single trigger for the Ratchet-Up tail. Watch which fires first. If the Section 122 lapse produces a smooth Section 301 handoff and the Busan truce renews, the 47% baseline hardens toward 55%+. If the Federal Circuit reverses and the truce collapses in the same window, the distribution inverts toward the tails faster than any 2028-dated forecast can comfortably absorb — and this chapter's medium confidence, not high, is the record that we said so in advance.
